## PART II: CANTOR FITZGERALD — GOVERNMENT BOND MARKET AND INTELLIGENCE INFRASTRUCTURE

### 2.1 Treasury Bond Market Dominance

Cantor Fitzgerald operated the world's first electronic marketplace for U.S. government securities (established 1972) and handled approximately 25% of all daily U.S. Treasury bond transactions by September 2001. The firm is one of 24 primary dealers authorized to trade directly with the Federal Reserve Bank of New York. As a primary dealer, Cantor participates in Treasury auctions, conducts open market operations with the Fed, and provides market intelligence to the Fed's trading desk.

**Intelligence Significance:** Any entity controlling a quarter of Treasury bond flow has structural visibility into sovereign debt issuance, Federal Reserve monetary operations, and large institutional trading patterns. This is not an allegation of intelligence activity -- it is a statement of the inherent intelligence value of the market position.

**[E1 | Confidence: 0.95]** Primary dealer status and market share are matters of Federal Reserve public record.

Sources:
- [Federal Reserve Bank of New York - Primary Dealers](https://www.newyorkfed.org/markets/primarydealers)
- [Cantor Fitzgerald - Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cantor_Fitzgerald)

### 2.2 eSpeed and 9/11 Infrastructure Destruction

**Claim:** The destruction of Cantor Fitzgerald's WTC offices eliminated critical financial infrastructure and transaction records.

**Primary Documentation:**
- Cantor Fitzgerald occupied floors 101-105 of the WTC North Tower, directly above the impact zone of American Airlines Flight 11.
- 658 of 960 New York employees were killed -- approximately 68% of the local workforce.
- 250 computer and server systems and 1,000 workstations were destroyed.
- Cantor's eSpeed electronic trading platform was obliterated.
- Despite the destruction, Cantor resumed taking orders for U.S. Treasury bonds through eSpeed within 47 hours.

**Analytical Note:** The research did not surface evidence that specific investigative records were targeted or that the destruction of Cantor's infrastructure served any intelligence purpose. The destruction was catastrophic and indiscriminate. However, the loss of transaction-level data from a firm handling 25% of Treasury volume is analytically significant for any investigation into pre-9/11 financial anomalies.

**[E1 | Confidence: 0.90]** Destruction details are extensively documented. The investigative implications are analytical inference [E3].

### 4.1 Pre-9/11 Suspicious Treasury Bond Trading

**Claim:** Unusual Treasury bond trading activity was detected before the September 11 attacks, and the CIA engaged British regulators to investigate.

**Primary Documentation:**
- Wall Street Journal reporters noted that the U.S. Secret Service contacted a number of bond traders regarding large purchases of five-year Treasury notes before the attacks. [E2]
- The CIA was reported to have asked British regulators to investigate some Treasury bond trades that occurred before the attacks. [E2]
- The 9/11 Commission and subsequent official investigations concluded that "every suspicious trade was determined to be part of a legitimate trading strategy totally unrelated to the terrorist attacks." [E1]

The destruction of Cantor's infrastructure 47 hours before trading resumed eliminated the most granular transaction-level data from the largest Treasury market participant. Whether this data loss was incidental to the attack or consequential for investigations cannot be determined from available evidence.

**[E2 | Confidence: 0.55]** Pre-attack suspicious trading is documented in investigative reporting and acknowledged by official investigations (which dismissed it). The Cantor-specific data-loss question is analytical inference [E3].
