## ANALYTICAL NOTES AND LIMITATIONS

1. **The Convergence Problem persists at the cross-campaign level.** Just as PKG-MK found that behavioral control techniques are independently discoverable, the ecosystem pattern identified across campaigns may reflect a universal property of institutional systems rather than a specifically American or CIA-originated phenomenon. The pattern may be inherent in any society with powerful institutions and weak accountability mechanisms.

2. **Evidence asymmetry.** PKG-EP has the strongest evidence base (838 claims, predominantly E1 financial records). PKG-MK has the broadest analytical scope but relies more heavily on E2-E3 for key chains (technique transfer, West's pattern). PKG-911 has the most focused scope but the shortest campaign. Cross-campaign confidence scores reflect the weakest link in each chain.

3. **The 1973 records destruction.** MK-Ultra records were destroyed on Richard Helms's order in 1973. This creates a permanent ceiling on confidence for any assessment involving direct MK operational connections. The ecosystem model is partly a response to this evidentiary constraint -- it describes what can be documented (techniques, institutions, personnel overlaps) rather than what cannot (classified operational files).

4. **Selection bias risk.** The Sherlock system is designed to find patterns and connections. There is an inherent risk that the system imposes pattern on noise. The independent convergence of three campaigns on the same structural conclusion mitigates but does not eliminate this risk. External validation would require testing the ecosystem model against domains NOT investigated by Sherlock.

5. **No living subjects were investigated for criminal prosecution.** All assessments are analytical, not accusatory. Confidence scores represent analytical probability assessments, not legal standards of evidence.
