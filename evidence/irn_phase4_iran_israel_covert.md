## Executive Summary

Iran and Israel maintained continuous covert cooperation from the founding of the Israeli state in 1948 through the 1979 Islamic Revolution and well beyond it — including during periods of maximal public hostility. This cooperation spanned intelligence sharing (SAVAK-Mossad, 1957-1979), joint military-industrial projects (Project Flower missile program, 1977-1979), a secret oil pipeline joint venture (EAPC, 1968-1979, with disputed $1.1 billion still owed to Iran), massive arms transfers during the Iran-Iraq War ($500M-$2B annually, 1981-1987), and Israel's initiating role in what became the Iran-Contra affair. After 1991, the relationship shifted from covert cooperation to overt strategic rivalry — but even this shift was driven by geostrategic repositioning (end of Cold War, defeat of Iraq) rather than ideology, as Trita Parsi's landmark research documents.

The key analytical finding: the Iran-Israel relationship from 1948-1991 was a textbook case of realpolitik cooperation masked by ideological posturing, directly supporting the cartel hypothesis at the regional level. The post-1991 rivalry represents genuine strategic competition, but one that operated through managed proxy conflicts until the escalation to direct war in 2025-2026. The February 2026 US-Israeli strikes and assassination of Khamenei represent a decisive break from the "managed rivalry" pattern — though the question of whether this was planned restructuring or genuine loss of control remains open.

## Key Findings

1. **The Peripheral Doctrine was a 30-year formal intelligence alliance (1948-1979)** — not ad hoc cooperation but structured, institutional partnership including Mossad training of SAVAK, joint Kurdish operations in Iraq, intelligence sharing, and weapons sales of ~$500M/year in the 1970s. (E1)

2. **Cooperation survived the Islamic Revolution intact** — Khomeini's regime maintained clandestine arms purchases from Israel throughout the Iran-Iraq War despite publicly denying Israel's right to exist. Iran needed weapons; Israel needed intelligence access and strategic depth against Iraq. Ideology was subordinated to strategic necessity. (E1/E2)

3. **Operation Seashell (1981) represents the largest documented case of an "enemy" state arming its declared adversary** — $75M in initial arms from IDF stockpiles, escalating to estimates of $500M-$2B annually through the mid-1980s. 350 Israeli technicians operated at Iranian airbases. (E1/E2)

4. **Israel initiated the Iran-Contra affair** — David Kimche (Foreign Ministry DG, former senior Mossad) approached McFarlane in July 1985, and PM Shimon Peres first suggested the contacts. Israel was not a passive conduit but the active architect of the arms-for-hostages channel. (E1 — Tower Commission)

5. **The EAPC oil pipeline represents a 50/50 joint venture that Israel nationalized and then refused to compensate** — a Swiss court ordered $1.1B payment to Iran; Israel refused. Israeli lawmakers voted to maintain secrecy over the company's operations, with disclosure classified as espionage. (E1)

6. **The 1991 inflection point was geostrategic, not ideological** — the Cold War's end and Iraq's defeat eliminated the two common threats (Soviet Union, Saddam) that had sustained Iran-Israel cooperation. The rivalry that followed was competition for regional preeminence, not religious war. (E2 — Parsi thesis)

7. **The 2003 "Grand Bargain" fax offered full resolution** — Iran proposed comprehensive normalization including acceptance of Israel and termination of Hamas/Hezbollah support. The Bush administration rejected it in favor of regime change. (E1 — documented diplomatic transmission via Swiss intermediary)

8. **The February 2026 US-Israeli strikes and Khamenei assassination broke the managed rivalry pattern** — whether this represents cartel restructuring or genuine loss of coordination control is the key open analytical question. (E1 — ongoing conflict)

## The "Managed Rivalry" Assessment

This is the key analytical output of Phase 4, directly testing the cartel hypothesis.

### Phase 1: Managed Cooperation (1948-1979) — Confidence: 0.95

The Peripheral Doctrine alliance was unambiguously managed cooperation. Mossad trained SAVAK. Joint military projects operated at the highest classification. Oil flowed through a secret pipeline. Weapons sales reached ~$500M/year. The cooperation was institutional, continuous, and strategically rational for both parties. Both sides concealed it from Arab states that were nominal allies/adversaries respectively. **This phase is fully documented and not in dispute.**

### Phase 2: Covert Cooperation Despite Public Hostility (1979-1991) — Confidence: 0.85

The Islamic Revolution's anti-Israel ideology was real at the public level, but the covert relationship continued essentially uninterrupted. Khomeini approved continued arms purchases. 350 Israeli technicians operated at Iranian airbases. Arms-for-oil barter continued. Israel initiated the Iran-Contra channel through Kimche-McFarlane. The "troika" (Nimrodi, Schwimmer, Kimche) managed the pipeline. Ahmad Kashani visited Israel to negotiate. **This phase represents the strongest evidence for "managed rivalry" — maximum public hostility coexisting with continuous covert cooperation.**

### Phase 3: Genuine Strategic Competition (1991-2023) — Confidence: 0.70

The 1991 inflection point was real. With the Soviet Union gone and Iraq defeated, the geostrategic rationale for cooperation evaporated. Iran and Israel became genuine competitors for regional preeminence. However, the competition was managed through proxy warfare (Hezbollah, Hamas) rather than direct conflict. The 2003 Grand Bargain offer suggests Iran sought a comprehensive settlement. The JCPOA process (2012-2015) demonstrates both sides maintained backchannel infrastructure for potential de-escalation. **The rivalry was genuine but operated within implicit boundaries — no direct military confrontation for 32 years.**

### Phase 4: Escalation to Direct War (2023-2026) — Confidence: 0.90

October 7, 2023 and its aftermath broke the managed competition framework. Iran's proxy network (Hamas, Hezbollah, Houthis) activated simultaneously. Israel's response expanded to direct strikes on Iranian territory (April 2024, June 2025). The February 2026 Operation Epic Fury — assassination of Khamenei, 900 strikes in 12 hours — represents an unambiguous break from managed rivalry. The escalation to nine-country Iranian retaliation suggests loss of escalation control on both sides.

### Analytical Verdict: GRADIENT

The Iran-Israel relationship is neither purely genuine rivalry nor purely managed competition. It has been a **gradient** that shifted over time:

| Period | Assessment | Confidence |
|--------|-----------|------------|
| 1948-1979 | **Managed cooperation** | 0.95 |
| 1979-1991 | **Managed rivalry** (public hostility, covert cooperation) | 0.85 |
| 1991-2023 | **Genuine rivalry** with managed escalation boundaries | 0.70 |
| 2023-2026 | **Unmanaged escalation** toward direct war | 0.80 |

**Cartel Hypothesis Implications:**

The 1948-1991 period strongly supports the cartel hypothesis at the regional level: two states that present as ideological enemies while maintaining continuous covert cooperation in intelligence, arms, oil, and military technology. This is precisely what a cartel maintenance operation looks like — competition optics masking coordination fundamentals.

The post-1991 period is more complex. The rivalry became genuinely strategic, but the 32-year avoidance of direct conflict (despite multiple crises) and the maintenance of permanent backchannel infrastructure (Oman, Switzerland) suggest residual coordination mechanisms.

The 2025-2026 escalation to direct war is the strongest counter-evidence against the "managed rivalry" thesis. Either: (a) the cartel lost coordination control (breakdown scenario), or (b) the war itself is a restructuring operation (the most aggressive cartel hypothesis). Current evidence does not resolve this question.

**Overall "Managed Rivalry" Confidence:** 0.65 (for 1948-2023 period); 0.25 (for current 2025-2026 period)
