## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Alan Courtney "Ace" Greenberg (September 3, 1927 – July 25, 2014) was the dominant architect of Bear Stearns' transformation from a mid-tier partnership into one of Wall Street's largest investment banks. As CEO 1978–1993 and Chairman 1985–2001, he oversaw growth from $46M to $1.8B in shareholders' equity and from 1,000 to over 6,300 employees. He died of cancer at age 86, having lived to see his life's work acquired in a fire sale for $10/share. His significance to the Epstein investigation is threefold: he personally hired Epstein at Bear Stearns in 1976, he or his immediate family facilitated the recommendation, and he maintained a warm post-departure relationship with Epstein through the firm's collapse in 2008.

**Military service gap**: No documented WWII or Korean War service found. Born 1927, graduated 1949 — age-eligible for Korea (1950–1953). This is an unresolved intelligence gap (see Section 5).

### 2.2 Relationship with Cy Lewis and the Firm's Culture

Greenberg's ascent at Bear was not smooth. He clashed repeatedly with managing partner Salim "Cy" Lewis, particularly over risk management — Greenberg advocated selling falling stocks while Lewis resisted. Despite this friction, Greenberg was mentored in arbitrage by John Slade (not Lewis) and rose through that department. When Lewis fell ill, Greenberg effectively ran operations; when Lewis died in May 1978, Greenberg formally assumed the CEO role.

### 2.4 The PSD Philosophy and Management Memos

Greenberg articulated his hiring and management philosophy in a celebrated series of internal memos, later published as "Memos from the Chairman" (1996). His core framework:

| Concept | Description | Evidence Tier |
|---------|-------------|---------------|
| PSD | "Poor, Smart, Deep desire to be rich" — his preferred profile for new hires over credentialed but complacent candidates | E1 | Memos from the Chairman; NW Recruiting Partners |
| Anti-nepotism | "We didn't allow any family at all to work at Bear Stearns — no son-in-laws, no children, no anything" (his own statement, PBS Frontline) | E1 | PBS Frontline Meltdown interview |
| Expense discipline | Famous memos instructing staff to reuse rubber bands, paper clips, envelopes; turn off lights; use miles for business travel; squeeze printer toner cartridges | E1 | Memos from the Chairman; Novel Investor |
| Mandatory philanthropy | Required all Bear Stearns executives to contribute 4% of their salaries to charity | E2 | InvestmentNews |
| Common sense over hierarchy | "Free your motivated, intelligent people from the chain of command" | E1 | Memos from the Chairman |
| Phone discipline | "Always return phone calls promptly and courteously" — recurring memo theme | E1 | Memos from the Chairman |

**Intelligence note:** The PSD philosophy is directly relevant to the Epstein hire. A no-college-degree, hyper-motivated 23-year-old math teacher fit the PSD template exactly. Greenberg's hiring of Epstein was consistent with his stated philosophy — not an anomaly. This makes the "unusual hire" framing in popular coverage somewhat misleading.

### 3.4 Reputation and Character

Greenberg was widely described as:
- **Mercurial and demanding** — frequently adversarial with subordinates who didn't meet his standards
- **Cost-obsessed** — the rubber-band memos were legendary and genuinely reflected his operating culture
- **Accessible** — kept an open office culture; answered his own phone; returned calls
- **Competitive** — bridge and magic hobbies reflect a personality drawn to mastery of complex systems
- **Fiercely Jewish-identified** — explicitly self-identified ("Everybody knows I'm a Jew," Tablet Magazine interview); channeled this into major Jewish philanthropic work
- **Loyal but retributive** — warm toward those he favored (including Epstein post-departure); publicly savage toward Cayne in his memoir

## 6. GOVERNMENT & REGULATORY INTERFACE

### 6.1 Bear Stearns as Primary Dealer

Bear Stearns held primary dealer status with the Federal Reserve, meaning it participated directly in U.S. Treasury securities auctions and had access to the Federal Reserve's discount window. This is a privileged position that places the firm at the intersection of government finance and private capital.

### 6.3 Intelligence-Relevant Regulatory Observations

Bear Stearns' primary dealer status means:
1. The firm had continuous formal relationships with the Federal Reserve Bank of New York
2. It participated in government financing mechanisms that required regulatory trust
3. It was subject to Fed oversight in ways other broker-dealers were not
4. The 2008 JPMorgan/Fed bailout structure ($30B in Fed support) was facilitated through this existing relationship

This context is relevant to understanding why Epstein's post-departure maintenance of Bear Stearns as his primary broker carried potential intelligence significance: Epstein's financial operations ran through an institution with direct Fed access and government securities market participation. The post-departure relationship (see Phase 2) was not merely a business convenience.
