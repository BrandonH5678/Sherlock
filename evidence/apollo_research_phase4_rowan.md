## 2. Athene Holding -- Rowan's Key Innovation

### 2.1 Founding and Structure

**Athene Holding Ltd.:**
- **Founded:** 2009 (E1 -- Athene corporate records)
- **Domicile:** Hamilton, Bermuda (E1)
- **Type:** Retirement services and annuity company
- **Initial capitalization:** Apollo committed initial capital; additional capital from institutional investors
- **Core business:** Acquiring and managing annuity and pension obligations

Athene was Rowan's vision and is widely credited as the single most important strategic innovation in Apollo's history. The concept:

1. Insurance companies and pension funds hold long-duration liabilities (annuity payments, pension obligations) that extend decades into the future
2. These liabilities must be matched with long-duration assets
3. Traditional insurers invest these assets conservatively (government bonds, investment-grade corporate bonds)
4. Rowan's insight: Apollo's credit expertise could generate higher returns on these same assets, through private credit, asset-backed securities, and other alternative credit instruments
5. By owning an insurance company (Athene), Apollo could access insurance company balance sheet capital on a permanent basis -- replacing the traditional PE fund model (raise, invest, return, repeat) with perpetual capital

(E1 -- Apollo SEC filings describing Athene strategy; E2 -- financial press analysis)

### 2.3 The Insurance Float Innovation

Rowan's core insight, explained in more detail:

**Traditional insurance model:**
- Insurance company collects premiums
- Invests premiums in conservative fixed income
- Pays claims from investment returns + reserves
- Excess returns = profit

**Rowan's model (Athene):**
- Athene acquires blocks of annuity/pension obligations from other insurers (often at a discount)
- Apollo manages the investment portfolio backing these obligations
- Apollo uses its credit expertise to invest in higher-yielding assets (private credit, structured credit, asset-backed securities)
- The yield differential between Apollo's portfolio and what traditional insurers earn = excess return
- Because insurance/annuity liabilities are long-duration and predictable, this capital is effectively permanent -- it doesn't need to be returned like a traditional PE fund

**Why this matters for Apollo's scale:**
- Traditional PE: Raise a fund every 3-5 years, invest, return capital. AUM fluctuates.
- Athene model: Permanently growing pool of investment capital that compounds over time. AUM grows continuously.
- This is why Apollo grew from ~$50B to $700B+ -- Athene provided the permanent capital engine. (E2 -- financial press analysis)

### 2.4 Bermuda Domicile: Tax and Regulatory Implications

Athene's Bermuda domicile is analytically significant:

**Tax implications:**
- Bermuda has no corporate income tax (E1 -- Bermuda tax law)
- Investment returns earned inside Athene's Bermuda entity are not subject to US corporate income tax until and unless they are distributed to US entities
- This creates a tax deferral advantage: Athene can compound investment returns without tax drag
- US policyholders are still subject to US tax on their annuity income, but the corporate-level tax advantage benefits Apollo/Athene shareholders (E2 -- tax analysis)

**Regulatory implications:**
- Bermuda insurance regulation is less stringent than US state insurance regulation in certain respects
- Capital requirements, risk-based capital ratios, and investment restrictions differ between Bermuda and US regulatory frameworks
- This has allowed Athene to hold a more aggressive investment portfolio than a comparable US-domiciled insurer could hold
- US state insurance regulators have expressed concern about the "Bermuda Triangle" of private equity-owned, offshore-domiciled insurance companies (E2 -- regulatory commentary)

**Government regulatory concerns:**
- The NAIC (National Association of Insurance Commissioners) has investigated the PE-owned insurance model, including Athene, for potential risks to policyholders (E1 -- NAIC proceedings)
- The US Treasury Department and Federal Reserve have studied systemic risk implications of the PE-insurance model (E2 -- policy papers)
- Congressional attention: Several Congressional hearings have addressed the PE-insurance model. Concerns include:
  - Whether policyholders bear excessive investment risk
  - Whether the Bermuda domicile evades appropriate US regulation
  - Whether the "permanent capital" model creates misaligned incentives (long-duration liabilities managed by performance-fee-driven PE firms)
- As of 2026, no significant regulatory action has been taken against Athene specifically, but the model remains under scrutiny (E2)

**PKG-911 relevance:** The Bermuda domicile connects to the broader theme of offshore financial structures in the Epstein network. Epstein extensively used USVI entities (Southern Trust, Financial Trust); Wexner used offshore structures; and now Athene (Rowan's creation) is domiciled in Bermuda. However, Athene's Bermuda domicile is a standard industry practice (many insurance/reinsurance companies are Bermuda-domiciled) and does not constitute evidence of impropriety. The parallel is structural, not operational. (E2)
