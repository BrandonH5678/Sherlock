## Executive Summary

The relationship between the Chinese Communist Party and Chinese secret societies/triads follows a distinctive arc: **strategic exploitation before power, ruthless suppression after power, selective re-engagement for overseas operations**. This pattern — instrumentalizing oath-bound criminal networks for state purposes — has direct structural parallels to Western intelligence agencies' documented use of organized crime (CIA-Mafia collaboration, Gladio stay-behind networks), though the Chinese model operates through a distinctive cultural vehicle: the Hongmen/Triad tradition stretching back to the 17th century.

The historical record is strongest for the pre-1949 period (E1-E2), where the Green Gang's role in the 1927 Shanghai Massacre and the CCP's own courting of the Gelaohui are extensively documented. The post-1949 suppression is equally well-documented (E1). The modern period — CCP re-engagement with triads through the United Front Work Department — is documented by a growing body of investigative journalism, government sanctions actions, and academic research (E1-E2 for key incidents like Yuen Long 2019; E2-E3 for the broader structural relationship).

**Key finding:** The CCP's relationship with organized crime is not a deviation from party doctrine but a recurring feature of its operational history, managed through the United Front framework that treats criminal networks as politically useful "patriotic" assets when they serve Beijing's interests abroad — particularly regarding Taiwan unification messaging, Belt and Road Initiative security, and diaspora control.

## Key Findings

### 1. The Green Gang and 1927 Shanghai Massacre (E1-E2)

The Green Gang (Qing Bang) represents the most thoroughly documented case of organized crime serving as the enforcement arm of political power in modern Chinese history. The "Big Three" — **Du Yuesheng** ("Big-Eared Du"), **Huang Jinrong** ("Pockmarked Huang"), and **Zhang Xiaolin** — controlled Shanghai's opium trade, gambling, prostitution, and extortion rackets from within the French Concession, where Huang served as chief of Chinese detectives in the French Concession police from 1892 to 1925.

Du Yuesheng's Sanxin Company held a monopoly on the opium business in the French Concession; at its peak, its profits equaled one-third of the Shanghai city government's total revenue (Wakeman, *Policing Shanghai*; Brian Martin, *The Shanghai Green Gang*).

On April 11, 1927, Chiang Kai-shek enlisted Du Yuesheng to eliminate the Communist-controlled labor movement. Du invited labor leader **Wang Shouhua** — himself a Green Gang member — to dinner at his residence, where Du's subordinate **Wan Molin** beat him, placed him in a sack, and buried him alive. The following dawn (April 12), Du's gangsters, wearing armbands marked "Labor" and operating under the front name "Chinese Progress Association," attacked Communist-controlled district offices across Zhabei, Nanshi, and Pudong. Estimates of dead range from 300 executed plus 5,000 missing (conservative) to 5,000-10,000 killed (broader estimates). Chiang subsequently awarded Du, Huang, and Zhang honorary ranks of Major General in the National Revolutionary Army.

**Analytical significance:** The 1927 Shanghai Massacre established the template — a political leader outsourcing political violence to organized crime networks that already controlled urban territory. This is structurally identical to the CIA's use of Corsican gangs in Marseille (1947), the Sicilian Mafia during Operation Husky (1943), and later Gladio stay-behind networks.

### 2. CCP's Own Use of Secret Societies Pre-1949 (E2)

The CCP's condemnation of the Green Gang's role in 1927 obscures its own parallel strategy of courting secret societies:

- **Mao Zedong's July 1936 Appeal:** Mao issued a formal appeal from the Central Committee to the **Gelaohui** (Society of the Elders), praising their anti-Qing tradition and urging them to join the anti-Japanese united front. He described certain secret societies as manifesting a Chinese "revolutionary spirit." Senior CCP leader **Zhu De** was himself a Gelaohui member.
- **Sun Yat-sen and the Hongmen:** Before the CCP's founding, Sun Yat-sen formally joined the Hawaiian branch of the **Chee Kung Tong** (Hongmen) in 1904. The Hongmen provided critical financial support for the 1911 revolution — funded partly through legal means and partly through brothels and opium sales. The Victoria, BC lodge mortgaged its building for the cause.
- **China Zhigong Party:** Founded in San Francisco in 1925 as a political offshoot of the Hongmen, the Zhigong Party joined the CCP-led United Front in 1947. It remains one of eight "democratic parties" in the PRC today, holding 39 NPC seats and 65 CPPCC seats — a formal, institutional bridge between the Hongmen tradition and the CCP state.

### 3. Post-1949 Suppression and Diaspora Displacement (E1-E2)

After establishing the PRC, the CCP reversed course decisively. The 1951 **Suppression of Counterrevolutionaries** campaign dealt violently with secret society leaders, religious associations, and KMT remnants. Secret societies were declared "feudal" and "reactionary." Those who did not disband were eliminated.

**Survival mechanism:** Geographic displacement. Triads migrated primarily to:
- **Hong Kong** (under British colonial protection) — became the global center of Triad activity
- **Taiwan** (under KMT, which had its own deep Triad ties through the Green Gang alliance)
- **Southeast Asian Chinese diaspora** communities

On the mainland itself, the CCP's revolutionary party apparatus replaced the social functions previously served by secret societies — mutual aid, dispute resolution, patronage networks. The Party became the only permissible oath-bound organization.

### 4. Deng Xiaoping and the "Patriotic Triads" Doctrine (E1-E2)

The decisive policy shift came from **Deng Xiaoping in October 1984**, who stated that the party was prepared to work with organized crime elements as long as they supported the country's interests. He noted that Chinese triads contained "patriotic elements," that "there are many good guys among them," and that triads had provided protection for senior Chinese officials overseas.

This was operationalized in the early 1980s when, at Beijing's behest, officials "befriended" Hong Kong's triad bosses ahead of the 1997 handover with an explicit bargain: China would turn a blind eye to their illegal activities if they promised to maintain stability after the transfer of sovereignty.

The doctrine was formalized publicly in **April 1993**, when **Tao Siju**, chief of China's Public Security Bureau, stated: "As long as these people are patriotic, as long as they are concerned with Hong Kong's prosperity and stability, we should unite with them." Days before this statement, Tao had opened the **Top Ten nightclub** in Beijing with **Charles Heung Wah-keung**, a Hong Kong entertainment mogul identified by law enforcement as a senior figure in the **Sun Yee On** triad.

### 5. Hong Kong Triads and CCP United Front Operations (E1-E2)

The three major Hong Kong triads — **Sun Yee On**, **14K**, and **Wo Shing Wo** — transitioned from anti-Communist organizations (14K was originally founded in 1945 by KMT General Kot Siu-wong in Canton with Nationalist allegiances) to CCP-aligned entities through the United Front framework.

Key documented connections:
- **Sun Yee On:** Tao Siju's direct partnership with Charles Heung (1993). Fujian Province CCP leadership partnered with Sun Yee On for human smuggling operations, according to sensitive U.S. government information (reported by McAdam).
- **14K:** Wan Kuok-koi ("Broken Tooth") — former 14K boss in Macau, imprisoned 1998-2012, now simultaneously a member of the **Chinese People's Political Consultative Conference** (CPPCC) and chairman of the **World Hongmen History and Culture Association**. Sanctioned by US Treasury under the Global Magnitsky Act in December 2020.
- **Wo Shing Wo:** Active in Yuen Long district; subgroups linked to the 2019 attack (see below).

### 6. The 2019 Yuen Long Attack — "Patriotic Gangs" in Action (E1)

On July 21, 2019, men in white shirts — identified by local councillors and police as having "triad backgrounds" — attacked passengers at **Yuen Long MTR station** with sticks, metal rods, and umbrellas, targeting pro-democracy protesters returning from a march. **45 people hospitalized**, one in critical condition. The attack occurred in two waves over approximately two hours.

**Critical police complicity evidence:**
- Hong Kong police did not arrive until **39 minutes after the attacks began**
- A detective sergeant from the **Organized Crime and Triad Bureau** had been monitoring a WhatsApp group where the attack was discussed **days in advance** (Radio Free Asia, November 2023)
- Police were subsequently shown on CCTV speaking cordially with white-shirted attackers and making no arrests at the scene
- The Yuen Long region has documented long-term 14K and Wo Shing Wo subgroup presence

**Analytical significance:** Yuen Long is the clearest modern documented case of the "patriotic gangs" phenomenon — triads providing extrajudicial violence against political opponents of Beijing, with apparent police acquiescence or coordination. This mirrors documented patterns in the PKG-EP campaign: the use of criminal networks for deniable political operations (cf. CIA-Mafia anti-Castro operations, Gladio P2-connected bombings).

### 7. The Macau Casino-Triad-CCP Nexus (E1-E2)

**Stanley Ho** held a government-granted monopoly on Macau gambling for 40 years through **STDM** (Sociedade de Turismo e Diversoes de Macau), commencing 1962. In the 1980s, Ho pioneered **VIP junket rooms** — private gambling spaces subcontracted to independent operators who brought high-rollers from the mainland. These junket operators were universally operated by or affiliated with triad societies, giving organized crime direct, structural access to casino revenue.

**Wan Kuok-koi ("Broken Tooth")** led the 14K during a violent turf war against the **Shui Fong** for control of these VIP rooms in the 1990s. He was arrested in May 1998 and convicted on charges of criminal association, loan sharking, and illegal gambling (15-year sentence). The violence ceased primarily because of the imminent 1999 Portuguese handover to the PRC — triad groups feared Beijing would eliminate their businesses.

Post-release (2012), Broken Tooth rebranded as a CCP loyalist while maintaining triad affiliations. His **World Hongmen History and Culture Association** in Cambodia became a vehicle for:
- Belt and Road Initiative (BRI) security contracting
- Cryptocurrency ventures
- Real estate and casino development
- The **Dongmei Zone** near the Thai-Burmese border (initially marketed as tourism, became a scam compound hub)

He was sanctioned by the US Treasury on December 9, 2020 (International Anti-Corruption Day) under the Global Magnitsky Act, along with three associated entities: the World Hongmen History and Culture Association, Dongmei Group (Hong Kong), and Palau China Hung-Mun Cultural Association.

### 8. Modern Global Hongmen Operations (E1-E2)

The 2025 **Jamestown Foundation** report by Martin Purbrick (former senior Hong Kong police intelligence officer) and the 2025 **Washington Post** investigation documented the global scope of CCP-Triad convergence:

- Wan Kuok-koi presides over the Hongmen with **300,000 members worldwide**, operating in Cambodia, Malaysia, Palau, Philippines, South Africa, Taiwan, Thailand, and Uganda
- In **Palau**, OCCRP documented a network of Chinese businesspeople with triad and CCP ties pushing online gambling operations on an island nation of 18,000 people
- In **Uganda**, Hongmen members established a **Chinese overseas police center** — a type of facility the FBI and rights groups identify as part of Beijing's transnational repression infrastructure
- In **Myanmar**, Broken Tooth established the Dongmei Zone scam compound complex through an agreement with the Karen Border Guard Force (2019)
- The OHCHR estimates **at least 220,000 people** from 40+ countries are held in scam centers in Cambodia and Myanmar alone, with 85,000 in Laos

**The operational model:** The CCP tolerates and enables Hongmen criminal activities (human trafficking, online scams, money laundering) provided operations remain outside China's borders and the organization remains loyal to CCP narratives — particularly on Taiwan unification, BRI promotion, and providing security for Chinese officials overseas.

### 9. Comparative Analysis: Chinese vs. Western Oath-Bound Networks (E2-E3)

| Dimension | Chinese Model (Hongmen/Triads) | Western Model (S&B/Masonic/Intelligence) |
|-----------|-------------------------------|------------------------------------------|
| **Origin** | Anti-Qing resistance (17th c.); class-based mutual aid | Enlightenment guild traditions (18th c.); elite social clubs |
| **Oath structure** | Blood oaths, 36 oaths of the Hongmen, death penalties for betrayal | Ritual initiation, secrecy oaths, social exclusion for betrayal |
| **State relationship** | Oscillates: exploitation → suppression → re-engagement | Persistent elite capture: members populate government continuously |
| **Personnel pipeline** | Reverse: state co-opts existing criminal networks | Forward: fraternity produces future state personnel (S&B → CIA, State Dept) |
| **Criminal nexus** | Explicit: opium, gambling, human trafficking are core businesses | Implicit: financial crimes, intelligence operations, occasionally explicit (CIA-Mafia) |
| **Current state function** | United Front: diaspora control, Taiwan messaging, BRI security | Deep state: intelligence community staffing, policy networks, financial system access |
| **Deniability mechanism** | "Patriotic" cover; cultural heritage organizations | "Private sector" cover; consulting firms, think tanks |
| **Geographic scope** | Chinese diaspora globally; BRI countries | Anglo-American; Five Eyes alliance; NATO countries |

**Key structural difference:** Western oath-bound networks (Skull & Bones, certain Masonic lodges) function primarily as **personnel pipelines** — they recruit promising individuals early, bond them through ritual, and insert them into positions of institutional power. The Chinese model is more transactional — the CCP co-opts **existing criminal organizations** and redirects their capabilities toward state objectives, without necessarily placing triad members in formal government roles (Wan Kuok-koi's CPPCC membership being a notable exception).

**Key structural similarity:** Both systems use **oath-bound loyalty as a mechanism of coercive control**. In both cases, the combination of shared secrets, mutual complicity in illicit activity, and hierarchical obligation creates networks that are resistant to external investigation and penetration. Both also exhibit the pattern of the state declaring these organizations officially suppressed or irrelevant while maintaining covert operational relationships.

### 10. The Zhigong Party as Institutional Bridge (E1)

The **China Zhigong Party** represents the formal, above-board institutional link between the Hongmen tradition and the PRC state. Founded in San Francisco in 1925, it joined the CCP-led United Front in 1947 and currently holds:
- 39 seats in the National People's Congress
- 3 seats in the NPC Standing Committee
- 65 seats in the CPPCC

Its membership consists primarily of returned overseas Chinese and their relatives. The Zhigong Party serves as the CCP's legitimate interface with Chinese diaspora communities — parallel to the Hongmen/Triad network that serves as the covert interface. This dual structure (overt party + covert criminal network) is functionally analogous to the documented pattern in Italian politics where the Christian Democratic Party served as the legitimate interface while Gladio/P2 and Mafia networks served the covert function.

## Analytical Assessment: The "Patriotic Criminality" Model

The evidence assembled in this phase reveals a distinctive Chinese model of state-organized crime relations that differs from but structurally parallels the Western patterns documented across Sherlock's PKG-EP, PKG-911, and PKG-MK campaigns:

**Phase 1 — Revolutionary Exploitation (pre-1949):** Both the KMT (via Green Gang) and the CCP (via Gelaohui, Hongmen) used secret societies as revolutionary infrastructure. Sun Yat-sen's formal Hongmen membership and Mao's 1936 Gelaohui appeal represent the Chinese equivalent of the OSS-Mafia partnership in WWII.

**Phase 2 — Post-Revolutionary Suppression (1949-1978):** The CCP eliminated all competitor oath-bound organizations on the mainland, monopolizing the loyalty structures that secret societies had previously provided. This has no direct Western parallel — Western democracies never attempted to suppress Masonic or fraternal organizations outright.

**Phase 3 — Strategic Re-engagement (1984-present):** Deng's "patriotic triads" doctrine and Tao Siju's 1993 operationalization created a framework strikingly similar to the CIA's documented approach: tolerate and enable criminal activity in exchange for operational services to state interests. The key difference is that the CCP's framework is semi-public (Deng and Tao made their positions known), while the Western equivalent operates through classification and deniability.

**Current operational status (2025-2026):** The Hongmen/Triad network now functions as a global arm of CCP influence, documented by the Jamestown Foundation, Washington Post, OCCRP, and US Treasury sanctions. The network's 300,000 claimed members across 8+ countries, involvement in BRI security, Taiwan unification messaging, overseas police stations, and scam compound operations represents an active, evolving state-crime partnership.

**Confidence in overall assessment:** 0.75 (probable). The historical record is strong (0.85-0.95). The modern CCP-triad operational relationship is well-documented for specific cases (Yuen Long E1, Wan Kuok-koi E1, Tao Siju E1) but the systematic nature of the relationship — whether it represents a coherent strategy or a collection of opportunistic arrangements — is assessed at 0.65-0.75.
