## Executive Summary

The period 2020-2026 represents the most concentrated institutional architecture-building since Bretton Woods (1944). Unlike previous restructuring episodes, the current period features **multiple competing architectural projects** running simultaneously rather than a single dominant framework. Key developments include: (1) central bank digital currency programs now active in 137 countries; (2) the EU's comprehensive AI Act entering enforcement; (3) digital identity infrastructure mandated across the EU by December 2026; (4) the WHO Pandemic Agreement adopted May 2025 (without US participation); (5) ESG/sustainability disclosure standards adopted by 21 jurisdictions; and (6) unprecedented asset concentration in three passive investment firms controlling ~25% of US corporate voting shares.

The WEF "Great Reset" narrative, while useful as a diagnostic of elite intent, has not produced a coherent implementation program.

**Confidence assessment:** This phase carries the highest analytical uncertainty in PKG-RESTRUCTURE. Evidence tiers are assigned conservatively.

## Key Findings

### 1. The WEF "Great Reset" — Proposal vs. Implementation Gap

Klaus Schwab and Thierry Malleret published *COVID-19: The Great Reset* (July 2020), proposing three pillars: (1) stakeholder capitalism replacing shareholder primacy; (2) ESG-driven sustainable development; (3) Fourth Industrial Revolution technology integration. The WEF's own Strategic Intelligence platform and Davos sessions promoted these themes extensively through 2021-2023.

**What was actually implemented:** Very little that can be directly attributed to the Great Reset initiative. The proposal contained no specific programs, timelines, or binding commitments. Individual components (ESG standards, digital identity, CBDC development) were already underway before the Great Reset was announced and continued on their own institutional trajectories. The Great Reset functions more as a **framing narrative** than an operational plan.

**WEF institutional collapse (2025-2026):** Schwab resigned as chairman in April 2025 amid whistleblower allegations of financial impropriety. His successor as CEO, Borge Brende (former Norwegian foreign minister), resigned in February 2026 after DOJ Epstein file releases revealed he attended three dinners at Epstein's New York residence in 2018-2019, communicated via email/SMS, and referred to Epstein as a "brilliant host." One dinner was also attended by Steve Bannon and a former Norwegian government minister. The WEF appointed Alois Zwinggi as interim CEO and is considering board reductions. This institutional crisis significantly undermines the WEF's capacity to drive any coordinated restructuring agenda.

**Cross-reference to PKG-EP:** Brende-Epstein connection validates the Sherlock campaign's hypothesis that Epstein network penetration extended into core global governance institutions, not just financial/academic ones.

### 2. CBDC Development — Bifurcated Architecture

**Global scope:** 137 countries (98% of global GDP) are exploring CBDCs. 49 pilot projects active worldwide. Three countries have fully launched (Bahamas, Jamaica, Nigeria).

**China (e-CNY):** The world's largest CBDC experiment. By November 2025: 3.4 billion transactions, ~$2.3 trillion processed, 2.25 billion wallets (12x growth from July 2024), operational across 17 provinces. China's e-CNY constitutes >95% of mBridge settlement volume.

**mBridge vs. Project Agora — the critical bifurcation:**
- **mBridge** (China, UAE, Thailand, Hong Kong, Saudi Arabia): Reached MVP in mid-2024. BIS withdrew in October 2024, citing "graduation" — but timing coincided directly with Putin proposing "BRICS Bridge" at the BRICS+ summit. $55.5 billion in cumulative settlements. Now operates independently of BIS oversight. Sanctions evasion concerns are the primary Western objection.
- **Project Agora** (Fed NY, Bank of England, Bank of Japan, ECB, and 40+ financial institutions including JPMorgan, Citi, HSBC, Swift): Launched April 2024, prototype phase 2025, results expected H1 2026. Explicitly described as a Western-anchored alternative. Focuses on tokenized wholesale central bank money integrated with commercial bank deposits.

**US anti-CBDC position:** The CBDC Anti-Surveillance State Act passed the House (219-210), prohibiting the Federal Reserve from issuing a retail CBDC. Trump's executive order codified this prohibition. The US position effectively blocks domestic retail CBDC while participating in wholesale cross-border experiments (Agora).

**Assessment:** Two parallel financial architectures are being constructed — one China-anchored (mBridge), one Western-anchored (Agora). This mirrors but does not replicate Cold War financial bifurcation. The critical difference is that mBridge participants include US allies (UAE, Saudi Arabia, Thailand), suggesting these systems may eventually interoperate rather than completely separate.

### 3. AI Governance — Three Competing Frameworks

**EU AI Act:** World's first comprehensive horizontal AI law. Entered force August 2024, enforcement phased 2025-2030. Risk-based classification system. Prohibitions on social scoring, real-time biometric surveillance (with exceptions), emotion recognition in workplaces/education. Mandatory requirements for "high-risk" AI systems.

**US approach (Trump administration):** EO 14179 (January 2025) revoked Biden's AI safety order (EO 14110). Shifted from risk-mitigation/oversight to deregulation/innovation-first. Removed mandatory red-teaming requirements. December 2025 EO on "Ensuring a National Policy Framework" further preempts state-level AI regulation. The US is explicitly prioritizing competitive advantage over safety regulation.

**China:** National TC 260 Cybersecurity released AI Safety Governance Framework (September 2024). AI Labeling Rules require explicit and implicit labels on AI-generated content. China's approach combines state control over content/ideology with permissive innovation in commercial applications.

**G7 Hiroshima Process:** Voluntary code of conduct for AI developers, announced October 2023. Directly influenced EU AI Act provisions. But remains non-binding, and US policy divergence post-January 2025 has undermined G7 coordination.

**Assessment:** AI governance is the domain where great power competition is most visible. The EU is setting compliance standards (regulatory export model similar to GDPR). China is building sovereign AI within state-controlled parameters. The US is pursuing unregulated innovation dominance. No unified global framework exists or appears likely.

### 4. Digital Identity Infrastructure

**EU Digital Identity Wallet (EUDI):** Under eIDAS 2.0 (Regulation 2024/1183), all 27 EU member states must provide functional digital identity wallets by December 2026. Beta testing underway in Italy, Germany, Sweden. Will store national ID, driving license, diplomas, professional qualifications, health records. Regulated sectors (banks, telecoms) must accept EUDI wallets by mid-2027. Implementation readiness varies widely — Netherlands signaling delays, Bulgaria reportedly not started.

**World Bank ID4D:** 800 million people globally still lack legal ID; 2.9 billion lack digital IDs. ID4D promotes "Digital Public Infrastructure" (DPI) — integrated identity systems linked to government payment systems (G2Px).

**Pandemic infrastructure persistence:** The WHO contracted T-Systems (Deutsche Telekom subsidiary) for a global vaccine certificate system based on QR code digital ID. The WHO Pandemic Agreement (adopted May 2025, 124 members) includes digital health certificate interoperability provisions, though explicit sovereignty protections state the WHO cannot "mandate or otherwise impose any requirements" including vaccination mandates. However, the digital infrastructure (verification systems, certificate standards) has persistence value beyond the pandemic context.

**US position:** Withdrew from WHO (formally January 2026). Not participating in WHO digital health certificate systems. No federal digital identity mandate.

**Assessment:** Digital identity infrastructure is being built at scale in the EU and developing world, but faces significant resistance in the US and is fragmenting along geopolitical lines. The pandemic did accelerate digital identity development, but "vaccine passport as permanent control mechanism" overstates the evidence — most vaccine passport programs have been discontinued, while the underlying digital identity infrastructure continues on its pre-existing trajectory.

### 5. The "Big Three" Asset Managers — Structural Power Analysis

**Scale:** BlackRock ($14T AUM), Vanguard ($9.3T), State Street ($4.3T). Combined >$25 trillion as of Q2 2025. Largest shareholder in >40% of publicly traded US firms and 88% of the S&P 500. Control ~25% of US corporate voting shares.

**Academic analysis:**
- Azar, Schmalz, Tecu (2018, *Journal of Finance*): Found common ownership in airlines increased ticket prices 3-12%. However, Azar's own later research found inter-industry effects may offset intra-industry anticompetitive effects.
- Bebchuk & Hirst (2019, *Columbia Law Review*): "Index Funds and the Future of Corporate Governance" — argued Big Three underinvest in stewardship, creating excessive deference to corporate management.
- Coates (Harvard): "Problem of Twelve" — warned that 12 management teams may gain practical power over majority of US public companies, constituting excessive concentration regardless of intent.

**2025 proxy season:** Big Three backed 98.7% of management-sponsored resolutions. Each firm has now split proxy voting into two teams — a response to political pressure from both ESG advocates and anti-ESG critics.

**BlackRock-sovereign wealth fund nexus:** BlackRock Riyadh Investment Management (BRIM) launched with $5B from Saudi PIF. Larry Fink maintains personal relationship with MBS (multiple private dinners). Abu Dhabi sovereign funds (ADIA, Mubadala) hold >$1.1B in BlackRock Bitcoin ETF (IBIT).

**Anti-ESG backlash:** 17 anti-ESG laws passed in Republican states in 2024. Texas initially banned BlackRock from state contracts, then removed BlackRock from boycott list in June 2025 after BlackRock withdrew from Net Zero Asset Managers Initiative and Climate Action 100+. BlackRock, State Street, JPMorgan, Pimco all withdrew from Climate Action 100+.

**Assessment:** The Big Three constitute a novel coordination mechanism in global capitalism — not through conspiracy but through structural position. Their voting power over 88% of the S&P 500 creates a de facto governance layer that no previous era had. However, the anti-ESG backlash and BlackRock's strategic retreat from climate commitments demonstrate that this power is politically contestable. The Big Three are adapting to political pressure rather than imposing a fixed agenda.

### 6. ESG as Compliance Architecture

**Standard-setting:** The IFRS Foundation's International Sustainability Standards Board (ISSB) issued IFRS S1 and S2 in June 2023. By January 2026, 21 jurisdictions adopted these standards (mandatory or voluntary). Mandatory reporting now required in Chile, Qatar, Mexico, Tanzania, Malaysia (phased). The ISSB absorbed the SASB Standards and builds on the TCFD framework.

**EU:** Corporate Sustainability Reporting Directive (CSRD) requires ~50,000 companies to report.

**US reversal:** SEC adopted climate disclosure rule March 2024, then abandoned its defense of the rule in March 2025 under the Trump administration. The US is now the major jurisdiction actively retreating from mandatory ESG disclosure.

**Assessment:** ESG disclosure is becoming a de facto global compliance standard through the ISSB/EU pathway, but US non-participation creates a significant gap. The ESG framework represents real compliance architecture — companies operating in ISSB-adopting jurisdictions must report regardless of US policy. However, BlackRock's retreat from climate commitments under political pressure demonstrates that ESG as an enforcement mechanism depends on political will, not just institutional design.

### 7. WHO Pandemic Agreement

The WHO Pandemic Agreement was adopted by consensus on May 20, 2025 at the 78th World Health Assembly. 124 members adopted; 11 abstained (including Poland, Israel, Italy, Russia, Slovakia, Iran). The US did not participate due to its WHO withdrawal.

**Key provisions:** Pathogen access and benefit-sharing system (PABS); pandemic prevention, preparedness and response framework; equity provisions for developing countries.

**Sovereignty protections:** The Agreement explicitly states it does not provide WHO authority to "direct, order, alter or otherwise prescribe" national law or mandate specific actions (bans, vaccination mandates, lockdowns).

**Assessment:** The Pandemic Agreement is a real institutional development but substantially weaker than initial proposals suggested. US non-participation fundamentally limits its scope. The sovereignty protections were added specifically to address the concerns that drove conspiracy narratives.

Key parallels: the dominant power (US then, US now) is withdrawing from multilateral institutions it helped create; competing institutional architectures are emerging (League vs. bilateral treaties then; Western vs. BRICS frameworks now); technological change is outpacing governance capacity; and the crisis catalyst has not produced a unified response.

### 9. Is This a "Managed Restructuring"?

**For the "managed" hypothesis:** There is real evidence of coordinated institutional design. The WEF's Great Reset, BIS CBDC coordination, ISSB standard-setting, G7 Hiroshima Process, and EU regulatory harmonization all represent deliberate architecture-building by identifiable actors. The personnel overlap is documented — BlackRock's Larry Fink at WEF and with Saudi MBS; central bankers rotating between BIS, national banks, and IMF; EU regulators setting global standards through Brussels Effect.

**Against the "managed" hypothesis:** The architecture is not unified — it is fragmenting. The US is actively undermining multiple pillars (WHO withdrawal, anti-CBDC legislation, ESG reversal, AI deregulation). China is building parallel systems (e-CNY, mBridge, domestic AI governance). The WEF — the institution most identified with coordination — is in institutional collapse (Schwab resignation, Brende-Epstein resignation, board reductions). The Great Reset produced no binding commitments.

**Assessment (conservative):** The current period is best characterized as **contested restructuring** (0.50-0.60 confidence) rather than managed restructuring (0.25-0.35) or purely organic/uncoordinated evolution (0.15-0.25). Multiple factions are attempting to shape post-crisis architecture, but no single faction has sufficient power to impose a unified design. This is analytically distinct from both the PKG-RESTRUCTURE cartel hypothesis (which implies coordination) and the mainstream narrative (which treats these as independent policy developments).

## Negative Findings

1. **Great Reset as operational program — NOT SUPPORTED.** Despite extensive conspiratorial interpretation, the Great Reset produced no binding commitments, no implementation timeline, and no enforcement mechanism. Individual components (CBDC, ESG, digital identity) were already in development before the initiative was announced and continued on independent institutional trajectories. The WEF's institutional collapse (2025-2026) further undermines any coordinated implementation claim. Evidence tier for "Great Reset as coordinated implementation plan": E3, confidence 0.15-0.20.

2. **Unified global CBDC — NOT SUPPORTED.** The CBDC landscape is bifurcating (mBridge vs. Agora), not unifying. The US is actively blocking domestic CBDC. No evidence of cross-faction coordination toward a single digital currency system.

3. **WHO as sovereign override mechanism — NOT SUPPORTED by treaty text.** The Pandemic Agreement contains explicit sovereignty protections prohibiting WHO from mandating specific national actions. However, the infrastructure for digital health certificates does have architectural persistence beyond stated purpose — this warrants monitoring (E2, 0.40-0.50 for "function creep" beyond pandemic context).

4. **"Managed restructuring" by unified elite — LOW CONFIDENCE.** Evidence shows factional competition, not coordination. The US is actively undermining multiple restructuring pillars that other factions are building. BlackRock's ESG retreat under political pressure demonstrates that even the most powerful institutional actors are responsive to political contestation, not unilaterally imposing agendas.

5. **Vaccine passport as permanent surveillance infrastructure — NOT SUPPORTED at scale.** Most vaccine passport programs have been discontinued. The EU digital identity wallet was in development before COVID-19 and follows an independent regulatory trajectory. However, pandemic-era infrastructure did accelerate digital identity timelines — the relationship is catalytic, not causal.

6. **Big Three as intentional coordination mechanism — OVERSTATED.** Academic evidence (Azar, Bebchuk, Coates) documents structural power, not intentional coordination. The Big Three's voting patterns (98.7% management alignment) may reflect passive deference rather than active direction. The "Problem of Twelve" is a structural feature of index fund capitalism, not evidence of conspiracy.

7. **Single architect of current restructuring — NOT IDENTIFIABLE.** Unlike Bretton Woods (White/Keynes) or Versailles (Wilson/Clemenceau/Lloyd George), no single individual or small group is demonstrably directing current architectural changes. The closest candidates — Schwab (resigned), Fink (retreating from ESG), Lagarde (ECB, limited to eurozone) — each control only fragments of the overall architecture.

## Analytical Assessment: Contested vs. Managed Restructuring

The PKG-RESTRUCTURE campaign hypothesis posits that "major regime changes, revolutions, and world wars are cartel maintenance operations." Applied to the current period:

**Evidence FOR cartel maintenance interpretation:**
- Institutional architecture is being designed by a narrow set of actors (BIS, WEF, Big Three, EU Commission, G7)
- Personnel circulate between these institutions (revolving door is documented)
- The architecture favors incumbent institutional power (CBDC controlled by central banks, ESG compliance benefits large firms, AI regulation barriers to entry)
- BlackRock-sovereign wealth fund integration suggests cross-factional capital coordination

**Evidence AGAINST cartel maintenance interpretation:**
- Architecture is fragmenting, not consolidating (mBridge vs. Agora, US vs. EU on AI/ESG/WHO)
- The WEF — the institution most associated with elite coordination — is in institutional collapse
- US domestic politics (anti-CBDC, anti-ESG, WHO withdrawal) are actively undermining restructuring pillars
- No single faction has implementation capacity across all domains
- The "Great Reset" — the most explicit coordination attempt — produced no binding outcomes

**Conclusion:** The current period is better modeled as **oligopolistic competition** (multiple powerful actors competing to shape architecture) than as **cartel coordination** (unified actors managing restructuring). This is consistent with the Prism game theory model's cartel confidence range of 0.65-0.75 — there are cartel-like features (shared interests in maintaining systemic stability, institutional overlap, personnel circulation) but insufficient coordination to constitute a true managed restructuring. The 2020-2026 period may represent a **transition between cartel regimes** — the post-Bretton Woods order is breaking down, and the successor order has not yet consolidated.
