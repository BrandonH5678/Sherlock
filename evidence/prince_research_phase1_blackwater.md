## 2. BLACKWATER'S FOUNDING AND EARLY GROWTH

### 2.1 Founding (1997)

Erik Prince founded Blackwater USA in 1997 in Moyock, North Carolina, a rural town in Currituck County near the Great Dismal Swamp, approximately 60 miles south of Naval Station Norfolk, Virginia.

| Founding Detail | Description |
|----------------|-------------|
| Original name | Blackwater USA (named after the dark, tannin-stained water of the surrounding swamp) |
| Location | 7,000-acre compound, Moyock, North Carolina (later expanded to ~7,000 acres) |
| Initial purpose | Private firearms and tactical training facility for military and law enforcement |
| Initial investment | Funded primarily from Erik Prince's inheritance from the Prince Corporation sale |
| Early focus | Training range, shooting facilities, tactical driving courses |
| Legal structure | Private company, wholly owned by Erik Prince through EP Investments LLC / Prince Group LLC |

**Why Moyock?** The location was strategic: rural enough for live-fire training with no civilian complaints, yet proximate to Naval Station Norfolk (largest naval base in the world), Joint Expeditionary Base Little Creek-Fort Story (SEAL Team headquarters), CIA's Camp Peary ("The Farm," approximately 90 miles away), and Fort Bragg (Special Operations Command, approximately 250 miles). Blackwater was positioned in the exact center of the East Coast special operations and intelligence community corridor. (E2 -- geography and military base locations; E3 -- inference about strategic positioning)

### 2.2 Pre-9/11 Operations (1997-2001)

Before September 11, 2001, Blackwater was primarily a training facility:

- Provided firearms and tactical training to federal, state, and local law enforcement
- Won early contracts with the U.S. Navy for training at Moyock
- After the USS Cole bombing (October 12, 2000, Aden, Yemen), the Navy contracted Blackwater to provide force protection training
- Revenue was modest by later standards -- the company was still primarily a training operation
- Prince was building relationships within the special operations and intelligence communities

**KEY PRE-9/11 FACT:** Blackwater was not yet a private military contractor (PMC) in the operational sense. It was a training company. The transformation from training facility to full-spectrum PMC happened entirely as a consequence of the September 11 attacks and the War on Terror. (E1 -- corporate records, pre-9/11 contract documentation)

### 2.3 Post-9/11 Transformation

### 2.4 What Blackwater Provided That Conventional Military Could Not

The fundamental value proposition that made Blackwater indispensable to the CIA, State Department, and DOD was not superior capability -- it was **deniability, speed, and freedom from congressional oversight**.

| Capability Gap | How Blackwater Filled It |
|---------------|------------------------|
| **Deniability** | Contractor casualties are not counted in official military casualty figures; contractor operations are not subject to the same congressional reporting requirements as military operations |
| **Speed of deployment** | Blackwater could put operators in a country within days; military deployments require congressional notification, logistics chains, basing agreements |
| **No congressional reporting** | Private contractors are not covered by the War Powers Act; their deployment does not trigger mandatory congressional notification timelines |
| **Legal ambiguity** | Contractors were not subject to the Uniform Code of Military Justice (UCMJ) until 2007 amendments; CPA Order 17 immunized them from Iraqi law |
| **Plausible distance** | If operations go wrong, the government can characterize contractors as independent actors rather than agents of the state |
| **Surge capacity** | Blackwater could scale rapidly using its pool of former SOF and military personnel without the political cost of increasing troop deployments |

**ANALYTICAL NOTE:** This capability gap is not incidental -- it is the entire business model. Blackwater existed because the U.S. government needed to conduct operations that it did not want to conduct through accountable military channels. The CIA's use of Blackwater for the assassination program is the clearest example: Congress was never notified because Blackwater was classified as a contractor, not a military unit. (E2 -- Congressional testimony; E3 -- analytical inference)
