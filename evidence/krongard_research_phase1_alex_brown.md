## 2. ALEX. BROWN & SONS AS AN INSTITUTION

### 2.1 Founding and Early History

Alex. Brown & Sons holds the distinction of being the **first investment bank in the United States**, founded in 1800 by Alexander Brown (1764-1834), an Irish linen merchant from Broughshane, near Ballymena, Ulster, who emigrated to Baltimore, Maryland. (E1 — Library of Congress records, Maryland Historical Society)

| Milestone | Year | Detail |
|-----------|------|--------|
| Founding | 1800 | Alexander Brown establishes firm in Baltimore |
| First US IPO organized | 1808 | Baltimore Water Company — first initial public offering in US history |
| Sons join firm | 1810 | William, George, John, and James Brown join; firm named "Alex. Brown & Sons" |
| Post-Panic pivot | 1837 | Withdrew from lending; focused on currency exchange, investment banking, international trade |
| Brown Brothers offshoot | 19th century | Brown family branches established Brown Brothers (later Brown Brothers Harriman — already Sherlock Target) |
| Continuous operation | 1800-1997 | 197 years of independent operation as oldest US investment bank |

**CROSS-REFERENCE NOTE:** Brown Brothers Harriman (BBH) is already in the Sherlock evidence base. The Brown family's 19th century financial dynasty produced both Alex. Brown & Sons (Baltimore) and Brown Brothers (New York), which merged with W. Averell Harriman's firm to form BBH in 1931. Prescott Bush was a BBH partner. This means the Brown financial dynasty connects to both the pre-9/11 put options story (through Alex. Brown → Deutsche Bank) and the Bush-Harriman-intelligence complex (through BBH). This genealogical connection merits further investigation. (E3 — inference from established genealogy; requires specific documentation of ongoing institutional links)

## 3. PRE-9/11 PUT OPTIONS — ALEX. BROWN AS EXECUTING INSTITUTION

### 3.1 Overview of Anomalous Trading

In the days immediately preceding the September 11, 2001 terrorist attacks, anomalous trading activity was detected in options on securities of companies that would be directly impacted by the attacks. The trading was concentrated in **put options** — contracts that increase in value when the underlying stock price declines — on:

- **United Airlines (UAL Corporation)**
- **American Airlines (AMR Corporation)**
- **WTC tenant companies** (Morgan Stanley, Merrill Lynch, Bank of America, Citigroup, JPMorgan)
- **Reinsurance companies** (notably Swiss Re)
- **Boeing**

This trading activity triggered immediate investigation by the Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC), the Federal Bureau of Investigation (FBI), and securities regulators in over a dozen countries. The International Organisation of Securities Commissions (IOSCO) reportedly characterized the investigation as relating to "the most important crime of insider trading ever committed." (E2 — multiple journalistic sources citing IOSCO characterization)

### 3.7 The 9/11 Commission Finding

The 9/11 Commission addressed the put options in **Footnote 130 of Chapter 5** and in **Staff Statement on Terrorist Financing (Appendix A)**:

**Official finding:** "Highly publicized allegations of insider trading in advance of 9/11 generally rest on reports of unusual pre-9/11 trading activity in companies whose stock plummeted after the attacks. Some unusual trading did in fact occur, but each such trade proved to have an innocuous explanation."

**Specific explanations offered:**
1. **UAL puts (Sept 6):** 95% purchased by "a single U.S.-based institutional investor with no conceivable ties to al Qaeda" as part of a broader trading strategy that also included buying 115,000 shares of American Airlines on September 10
2. **AMR puts (Sept 10):** Partially traced to a US-based options trading newsletter faxed to subscribers on Sunday, September 9, which recommended these trades

**Critical analytical gaps in the Commission's finding:**
- The identity of the "single U.S.-based institutional investor" was never publicly disclosed
- When FOIA requests were filed seeking the documentary evidence behind Footnote 130, the **SEC responded that potentially responsive records had been destroyed** (E1 — SEC FOIA response)
- The Commission's framing focused exclusively on whether traders had "ties to al Qaeda" — it did not assess whether non-al-Qaeda actors (e.g., intelligence operatives, organized crime, or others with foreknowledge) might have placed the trades
- The "innocuous explanation" for the UAL puts — that the investor also bought American Airlines shares — is a hedging strategy that is itself consistent with informed trading (hedging a foreknowledge-based short position with a long position to reduce suspicion)

Evidence tier: E1 (9/11 Commission Report text); E3 (analytical critique of the Commission's methodology)

### 3.8 Peer-Reviewed Statistical Analyses

Two peer-reviewed academic studies have challenged the Commission's "innocuous" finding:

#### Poteshman (2006)

| Field | Detail |
|-------|--------|
| Author | Allen M. Poteshman, Professor of Finance, University of Illinois at Urbana-Champaign |
| Title | "Unusual Option Market Activity and the Terrorist Attacks of September 11, 2001" |
| Published in | The Journal of Business, Vol. 79, No. 4, pp. 1703-1726 (July 2006) |
| Methodology | Computed daily option market volume statistics from January 2, 1990 through September 4, 2001 as baseline; compared against pre-9/11 trading |
| Key finding (UAL) | Put buying volume on UAL was "unusually high" — consistent with informed trading |
| Key finding (AMR) | Evidence "with about 99% probability" that American Airlines options were subject to insider trading |
| Overall conclusion | "An indicator of long put volume was unusually high which is consistent with informed investors having traded in the option market in advance of the attacks" |
| Evidence tier | E1 — peer-reviewed, published in top-tier finance journal (University of Chicago Press) |

#### Chesney, Crameri, and Mancini (2011/2015)

| Field | Detail |
|-------|--------|
| Authors | Marc Chesney, Remo Crameri, Loriano Mancini (University of Zurich / Swiss Finance Institute) |
| Title | "Detecting Abnormal Trading Activities in Option Markets" |
| Published in | Journal of Empirical Finance, Vol. 33, pp. 263-275 (2015); working paper versions 2011 |
| Methodology | Developed econometric method to detect "abnormal trades" — characterized by unusually large increments in open interest, trading volume, and option returns, not used for hedging |
| Airlines finding | Confirmed unusual activity in American Airlines and United Airlines puts |
| Banking sector finding | Five informed trading activities in Bank of America, Citigroup, JPMorgan, and Merrill Lynch |
| Banking sector gains | Approximately $11 million in realized gains |
| Swiss Re finding | Informed option trade on August 20, 2001 — return of 4,050% in three weeks (~EUR 8 million) |
| Boeing finding | Included in broader pattern of informed trading |
| Total estimated informed trading gains | ~$15 million across all identified companies |
| Overall conclusion | "The probability that crimes by insiders (insider trading) occurred is very strong" for American Airlines, United Airlines, Merrill Lynch, Bank of America, Citigroup, and JPMorgan |
| Evidence tier | E1 — peer-reviewed, Swiss Finance Institute / Journal of Empirical Finance |

**ANALYTICAL ASSESSMENT:** Two independent peer-reviewed studies using different statistical methodologies both concluded that the pre-9/11 options trading was consistent with informed trading at confidence levels well above 95%. The 9/11 Commission's "innocuous" characterization is directly contradicted by the academic literature. This does not prove who placed the trades or that the trades represented foreknowledge of the specific attacks — but it strongly indicates that *someone* was trading on material non-public information. Confidence: 0.85 that informed trading occurred; 0.40 that informed trading was foreknowledge-based (the academic studies cannot distinguish between attack foreknowledge and other forms of material information).
