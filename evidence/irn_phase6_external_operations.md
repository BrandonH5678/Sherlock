## Executive Summary

Iran operates the most extensive state-directed proxy and covert operations network outside of the United States, projecting power across the Middle East, Africa, Latin America, Europe, and Asia through the Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps-Quds Force (IRGC-QF), the Ministry of Intelligence and Security (MOIS), and a constellation of non-state proxy forces. Under Qasem Soleimani (1998-2020), the Quds Force was structured as a combined CIA/JSOC equivalent — integrating intelligence, paramilitary operations, political influence, financial networks, and assassination capability under unified command. Post-assassination, successor Esmail Qaani has maintained structural continuity but lost Soleimani's charismatic operational leadership, and the network suffered devastating blows in 2024-2025 with the degradation of Hezbollah's leadership cadre by Israel.

The network's financial architecture is remarkable in its diversity: Iranian state funding ($700M-$1B annually to Hezbollah alone), narcotrafficking (DEA-estimated $400M/year in Hezbollah drug proceeds), diamond smuggling, counterfeit goods, and sophisticated money laundering spanning the Tri-Border Area of South America, West Africa, and global Lebanese diaspora communities. Project Cassandra (DEA, 2008-2016) documented this architecture in detail but was reportedly constrained by political considerations related to JCPOA negotiations — a finding with significant implications for understanding how intelligence equities are traded against diplomatic objectives.

The structural parallel to CIA operations is the KEY analytical finding of this report. Both organizations: run proxy armies with deniability, conduct targeted assassinations, operate global financial networks outside normal banking, use diplomatic cover for intelligence operations, maintain cyber warfare capabilities, and integrate paramilitary action with political influence. The primary structural difference is scale (CIA's budget is ~20x larger) and the degree of institutional accountability (CIA operates under congressional oversight; Quds Force answers only to the Supreme Leader).

## Key Findings

### 1. Quds Force Organizational Structure Under Soleimani

Under Soleimani's command (1998-2020), the Quds Force functioned as Iran's primary instrument of extraterritorial power projection. U.S. Army General Stanley McChrystal described it as analogous to "a combination of the CIA and JSOC." Another U.S. official called it "a CIA, Pentagon, and State Department all rolled into one."

**Functional Branches (5):** Soleimani created five branches, each with a separate commander operating under a Council of Commanders with Soleimani at the apex:
- Intelligence
- Finance
- Politics
- Sabotage
- Special Operations

**Regional Commands:** Separate departments organized by country/region, each with a commander accountable only to Soleimani:
- Iraq (highest priority under Soleimani)
- Lebanon/Hezbollah liaison
- Syria
- Yemen
- Afghanistan (Fatemiyoun Division — Afghan Shia fighters)
- Africa
- Palestine (Hamas/PIJ coordination)

**Specialized Units:**
- **Unit 400** — Elite special operations; assassinations, kidnappings, sabotage against Western targets outside Iran
- **Unit 840** — Branched from Unit 400 in recent years; dedicated to foreign operational cells for assassination/kidnapping against Western targets and Iranian opposition. Divisions include: General Command, Intelligence Division, Middle East/Africa Division, Caucasus/Asia Division, Europe/US Division, and anti-dissident operational division
- **Intelligence-Gathering Department** — Collection and distribution across units
- **Mapping Department** — Operational planning
- **Political Department** — Analysis of parties and political figures in target countries
- **Operations Department** — Planning of kinetic activities

**Analytical Assessment:** This structure is functionally identical to CIA's Directorate of Operations division into regional divisions (Near East, Europe/Eurasia, etc.) with cross-cutting functional capabilities (counterterrorism, counterproliferation). The key difference: Soleimani exercised direct personal command over all regional commanders — the CIA Director does not personally direct field operations to this degree.

### 2. Post-Soleimani Transition (2020-Present)

Esmail Qaani was appointed hours after Soleimani's death on January 3, 2020. He had served as deputy commander for approximately two decades, with primary expertise in Afghanistan and Pakistan (eastern front), in contrast to Soleimani's Arab-theater orientation.

**Key Changes:**
- **Leadership style:** Bureaucratic/administrative vs. Soleimani's charismatic field leadership
- **Strategic continuity:** Khamenei publicly stated the force would follow an "identical" strategy
- **Structural continuity:** Organizational architecture largely maintained
- **Reduced influence:** Qaani widely assessed as having less personal influence with proxy leaders
- **Iraqi coordination:** The Iraqi Resistance Coordination Commission (IRCC) was created post-Soleimani/Muhandis killings to consolidate control over PMF groups — a structural adaptation to the loss of personal relationships
- **2024-2025 crisis:** Qaani was rumored to be under investigation for intelligence leaks that enabled Israel's devastating strikes on Hezbollah leadership. He was declared dead by multiple outlets during the Iran-Israel conflict before reappearing publicly in June 2025.

**Analytical Assessment:** The post-Soleimani Quds Force demonstrates the vulnerability of personality-dependent command structures. Soleimani was irreplaceable — his personal relationships with proxy leaders (Nasrallah, Muhandis, etc.) were the command-and-control architecture. Qaani has attempted to institutionalize these relationships through formal coordination mechanisms (IRCC), but the network has measurably degraded, particularly after Israel's 2024 decapitation strikes on Hezbollah.

### 3. Hezbollah Financing Mechanisms

Hezbollah's annual operating budget is estimated at approximately $1 billion (multiple sources). Funding sources:

**Iranian State Funding (60-70%):**
- $400M between 1983-1989 (early establishment phase)
- Estimated $700M annually by 2018 (Israeli security assessment: ~90% of budget)
- Post-2024 status uncertain — Israeli strikes on Hezbollah and degradation of Iranian financial channels may have reduced this

**Criminal Enterprise Revenue (30%):**
- **Narcotrafficking:** DEA affidavit (2016, Florida court) estimated $400M/year in drug proceeds. Cocaine routes from Latin America to Europe via West Africa. Captagon production in Syria/Lebanon.
- **Money laundering:** Used car export schemes (Latin America to Africa), trade-based money laundering, bulk cash smuggling
- **Counterfeit goods:** Cigarettes, luxury items, counterfeit U.S. currency
- **Diamond trade:** Democratic Republic of Congo illegal diamond operations; West African smuggling networks
- **Charcoal and oil smuggling**

**Diaspora Fundraising:**
- Lebanese Shia diaspora in West Africa, United States, Tri-Border Area
- Mix of voluntary donations, charitable front organizations, and coerced contributions

**Project Cassandra (DEA, 2008-2016):**
- Launched to track hundreds of millions flowing from North/South America through Europe and West Africa to Lebanon
- Identified Hezbollah's "Business Affairs Component" as a transnational criminal network
- Former Treasury official Katherine Bauer testified (House Committee on Foreign Affairs, Feb 2017) that law enforcement against Hezbollah was "constrained by policy decisions related to Iran"
- Investigation reportedly shut down just as it was targeting senior Hezbollah figures
- Obama administration officials denied blocking for political reasons, citing broader de-escalation objectives and JCPOA negotiations
- Post-JCPOA (2018), Trump DOJ established Hezbollah Financing and Narcoterrorism Team to resume enforcement

### 4. Iraqi Militia Coordination (PMF/Hashd al-Shaabi)

The Popular Mobilization Forces (PMF) comprise approximately 67 primarily Shia armed factions, formally part of the Iraqi Armed Forces and reporting to the Prime Minister, but in practice operating under Iranian direction.

**Command and Control Mechanisms:**
- PMF chairman Falih al-Fayyadh cooperates with IRGC to implement Iranian instructions
- Post-Soleimani/Muhandis: Iraqi Resistance Coordination Commission (IRCC) created to maintain cohesion
- Key Iran-aligned groups: Kataib Hezbollah (KH), Asa'ib Ahl al-Haq (AAH), Badr Organization
- Dual command: Formal Iraqi chain of command + informal Iranian direction through IRGC-QF advisers

**Influence Mechanisms:**
- Direct funding and weapons supply from Iran
- Training by IRGC-QF personnel (often embedded)
- Political patronage — PMF-affiliated parties hold seats in Iraqi parliament
- Social services provision — parallel governance in areas of control
- Economic enterprises — PMF groups control checkpoints, collect taxes, run businesses

**Analytical Assessment:** The PMF represents Iran's most successful proxy integration — formally embedded in a sovereign state's military while operationally responsive to Tehran. This is structurally analogous to CIA's relationship with friendly intelligence services during the Cold War, where formal sovereignty masked operational direction.

### 5. Houthi Support Networks

**Arms Supply Chain (UN Panel of Experts documentation):**
- Complete Iranian weapons systems (off-the-shelf) supplied via overland routes through Oman and by sea along Yemen's southern coast
- 2015-2018 interdictions: 37.5% of intercepted items identified as Iranian origin (vs. Russia 15%, China 5%)
- Components sourced from global supply chain: Japan (UAV/WBIED parts), China (UAV parts), Iran (UAV engines, RPGs), Belarus (optical sights), Czech Republic (fuel components), Germany (UAV engines)
- Intermediary network spans Hong Kong, Bangkok, Athens, Tehran, Muscat, Abu Dhabi
- Dual-use components flow continues, primarily originating in China

**Operational Guidance:**
- IRGC and Hezbollah advisers travel to Yemen to build capabilities and oversee attacks
- Technology transfer: drone and missile programs that have enabled Red Sea shipping attacks (2023-present)
- Operational coordination through IRGC-QF regional command

**Recent Development (2025):** Iran-Belarus covert arms network uncovered allegedly supplying weapons to Houthis — indicating supply chain adaptation in response to interdiction efforts.

### 6. Hamas Relationship

**Funding:**
- U.S. State Department (2020): Iran provides ~$100M annually to Palestinian militant groups including Hamas
- Israeli media (2018): Iran transferring ~$70M/year ($5.83M/month) specifically to Hamas
- Funding fluctuated significantly — reduced during Syrian civil war when Hamas supported Sunni opposition, then restored

**Training and Operational Support:**
- Hamas commanders personally trained by Soleimani in Iran
- Iranian-engineered missiles used in 2008-2009 Gaza War
- Arms funneled through Sudanese ports and Egyptian Sinai tunnels
- Hezbollah serves as intermediary for training (Unit 3800 — Palestine operations)

**The Sunni-Shia Bridge:**
- Relationship is a "marriage of convenience" — shared anti-Israel objective transcends sectarian divide
- Iran uses Hamas/PIJ support to market itself as pan-Islamic (not merely Shia) power
- Palestinian Islamic Jihad (PIJ) is closer to Tehran ideologically than Hamas
- Hamas maintains independence — described by CFR as "no Iranian puppet"
- Relationship strained when Hamas supported Syrian opposition (2011-2017), demonstrating limits of Iranian control
- Post-October 7, 2023: relationship dynamics under intense scrutiny

**Analytical Assessment:** Hamas demonstrates the limits of Iran's proxy model when applied across the Sunni-Shia divide. Unlike Hezbollah (ideological alignment + financial dependence = high control), Hamas retains significant autonomy because: (a) it has alternative funding sources (Gulf states, diaspora); (b) its Sunni identity creates ceiling on integration; (c) it has its own governance responsibilities in Gaza. This maps to CIA's experience with ideologically misaligned proxies (e.g., Afghan mujahideen factions that cooperated tactically while maintaining independent agendas).

### 7. Hezbollah's Global Financial Operations

**Tri-Border Area (TBA — Argentina/Brazil/Paraguay):**

**West Africa:**
- Long-standing smuggling network for contraband diamonds and commodities
- Democratic Republic of Congo — Hezbollah-connected traders in illegal diamond trade
- Revenue channeled back to Lebanon through informal value transfer systems (hawala)
- Now integrated with cocaine trafficking route: Latin America → West Africa → Europe/Middle East
- Used car export schemes serve as money laundering vehicles

**External Security Organization (Unit 910/ESO):**
- Headed by Talal Hamiyah; operates under direct Hezbollah senior leadership and IRGC-QF coordination
- Strictly compartmentalized from other Hezbollah units
- Operational presence in South America, Africa, Asia, and Europe
- Recruits from Lebanese diaspora communities and local converts
- Responsible for "planning, preparation, and execution of intelligence, counterintelligence, and terrorist activities" outside Lebanon
- Documented operational cells in Colombia, Cyprus, Bulgaria, Thailand, Peru, Bolivia

## The CIA Comparison: Structural Parallels in Global Covert Operations

This is the central analytical contribution of this phase. Both Iran's Quds Force network and the CIA's operations directorate represent state-directed global covert action architectures. The parallels are structural, not moral — understanding them illuminates how great and regional powers project extraterritorial influence through deniable means.

### Parallel Structure Matrix

| Function | IRGC-Quds Force | CIA Operations Directorate |
|----------|----------------|---------------------------|
| **Proxy armies** | Hezbollah, PMF, Houthis, Hamas/PIJ | Mujahideen, Contras, Hmong, Gladio networks, Syrian rebels |
| **Assassinations** | Unit 400/840 (documented: Mykonos, global plots 2018-2025) | Documented: Lumumba, Diem, Castro attempts; post-9/11 drone program |
| **Covert financial networks** | Hezbollah's TBA/West Africa/diamond networks | BCCI (E1 CIA banking relationship), Iran-Contra, drug trade allegations |
| **Diplomatic cover for intelligence** | Embassies as MOIS/IRGC platforms (12+ documented countries) | CIA stations under diplomatic cover (global) |
| **Regime change operations** | Iraqi politics, Lebanese politics, Syrian intervention | Iran 1953, Guatemala 1954, Chile 1973, Iraq 2003 |
| **Cyber operations** | APT33/34/35, critical infrastructure attacks | Stuxnet (joint with Israel), NSA/CIA cyber programs |
| **Political influence** | PMF parliamentary seats, Hezbollah governance | Funding democratic parties, labor unions (Cold War); NED post-1983 |
| **Training foreign forces** | IRGC trains proxy militias across region | CIA trains partner forces (Afghan commandos, Syrian opposition) |
| **Media/propaganda** | Al-Manar, Press TV, social media influence | Radio Free Europe, Congress for Cultural Freedom, modern IO |

### Key Structural Differences

1. **Scale:** CIA's annual budget (~$15-18B estimated) dwarfs Quds Force (~$1-2B estimated). This 10-20x differential is reflected in global reach.

2. **Accountability:** CIA operates under congressional oversight (Intelligence Committees, Inspector General, FISA Court). Quds Force answers only to the Supreme Leader. This is a fundamental structural difference — though CIA oversight has historically been variable in its effectiveness.

3. **Ideological Integration:** Quds Force proxies are bound by Shia revolutionary ideology (with Hamas as the notable Sunni exception). CIA proxies during the Cold War were bound by anti-communism — equally capable of producing ideologically misaligned partners (Afghan mujahideen → Taliban).

4. **Proxy Control Spectrum:** Iran exercises higher control over Hezbollah (ideological + financial dependence) than the CIA typically achieves over proxies. But both face the same fundamental challenge: proxies develop their own agendas. CIA's Contras committed human rights abuses that undermined political objectives. Iran's PMF groups sometimes act against Iranian interests in Iraqi domestic politics.

5. **Assassination Doctrine:** Post-Church Committee (1976), the CIA operates under Executive Order 12333 prohibiting assassination — though this was effectively circumvented by the post-9/11 drone program under "targeted killing" doctrine. Iran has no equivalent prohibition and conducts assassinations more openly through Unit 400/840.

6. **Financial Architecture:** Both developed parallel financial systems outside normal banking. CIA used BCCI (E1), front companies, and proprietary airlines (Air America). Hezbollah/Quds Force use narcotrafficking, diamond smuggling, and diaspora networks. The Project Cassandra case illustrates how intelligence equities can protect financial networks from law enforcement — the same dynamic documented in CIA's relationship with BCCI.

### Convergence Point: Project Cassandra and Intelligence Equity Trading

The Project Cassandra case is analytically significant because it demonstrates a universal dynamic: intelligence/diplomatic equities being prioritized over law enforcement against covert financial networks. The Obama administration's alleged constraint of DEA operations against Hezbollah to preserve JCPOA negotiations is structurally parallel to:
- Reagan administration protection of Contra drug trafficking to preserve the anti-Sandinista program
- CIA's protection of BCCI relationships despite knowledge of criminal activity
- Multiple administrations' protection of Saudi intelligence relationships despite evidence of terrorism financing

This is not a partisan observation — it is a structural feature of how states manage competing priorities between intelligence collection, diplomatic objectives, and law enforcement.

## Negative Findings

1. **No evidence of centralized Quds Force command post-2024 Israeli strikes.** The degradation of Hezbollah's leadership cadre and Qaani's uncertain status suggest the command architecture may be more fragmented than at any point since the network's establishment. Assessed as significant gap rather than negative finding — absence of evidence reflects operational security, not evidence of absence.

2. **Hamas operational independence from Iran is well-documented.** The "Iranian puppet" framing is NOT supported by evidence. Hamas retained significant autonomy, supported Syrian opposition against Iranian wishes (2011-2017), and maintains independent funding sources. Iran-Hamas is a patron-client relationship, not a command relationship.

3. **Project Cassandra "shutdown" is disputed.** Former Obama officials deny politically motivated blocking. The evidence supports CONSTRAINTS on enforcement (Bauer testimony, E1), but a deliberate "shutdown" is an editorial characterization, not a proven finding. Confidence in political interference: 0.70. Confidence in deliberate "shutdown": 0.45.

4. **Quds Force budget estimates are unreliable.** Published estimates range from $1B to $20B+ (the higher figures conflating IRGC-wide with Quds Force-specific spending). No independently verified figure exists. All budget comparisons with CIA should be treated as approximate.

5. **"Axis of Resistance" is not a unified command structure.** It is better understood as a hub-and-spoke network with Iran at the center, variable levels of control over each proxy (Hezbollah: high; Houthis: moderate; Hamas: low), and significant internal tensions. The term itself is Iranian strategic messaging, not an analytical descriptor.

6. **No confirmed evidence that Qaani was a spy or executed.** Despite persistent rumors (2024-2025) of investigation for intelligence leaks and reports of execution, Qaani reappeared publicly in June 2025. The rumors may reflect internal IRGC factional conflict rather than actual espionage.

7. **Hezbollah narcotrafficking scale is contested.** The $400M/year DEA figure comes from a single 2016 affidavit. Independent verification of this specific number is lacking. The existence of Hezbollah narcotrafficking is E1; the precise scale is E2-E3.
