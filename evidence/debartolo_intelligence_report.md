## CRITICAL FINDINGS — TOP 10

### Finding 1: Five Separate Law Enforcement Agencies Documented Organized Crime Connections (E2)

The DOJ (1970), US Customs (1981), FBI (1985), Pennsylvania Crime Commission, and Columbus Police Department (1991) all independently generated intelligence characterizing DeBartolo as an organized crime associate. This multi-agency convergence, spanning two decades and five institutions that do not share intelligence in real-time, is the strongest available indicator that law enforcement genuinely believed the organized crime connections were real rather than fabricated.

**Named organized crime associates:** Santos Trafficante Jr. (Tampa boss), Meyer Lansky (national syndicate financial architect), Carlos Marcello (New Orleans boss), Ronald Carabbia (Cleveland family Youngstown representative), Genovese-LaRocca crime family of Pittsburgh.

**Evidence tier: E2 (confirmed across multiple independent agencies)**

### Finding 2: Metropolitan Bank of Tampa — Offshore Financial Structure (E2)

DeBartolo acquired majority control of Metropolitan Bank of Tampa in 1975 — in Santos Trafficante Jr.'s home city. He had such control that he unilaterally forced the bank president's resignation in 1981. The bank operated through WFC Corporation / Grand Cayman subsidiary, a structure consistent with professional money laundering in this era. The bank failed in February 1982 (largest Florida failure at the time) with 40% loan delinquency. The Florida Department of Law Enforcement testified before Congress: "We didn't find any evidence of narcotics laundering per se, but what we did find shocked us." The Florida comptroller was investigating "what appears to be spurious loans" through the Grand Cayman chain.

**Evidence tier: E2 (structure documented; narcotics laundering not proven)**
**Significance:** A mob-connected businessman from Youngstown controlling a Tampa bank with offshore Grand Cayman routing, in Trafficante's city, during the peak narco-trafficking period, is the most documentable potential financial structure in DeBartolo's organized crime file.

### Finding 3: Ronald Carabbia Relationship — FBI-Documented Social Relationship (E2)

FBI surveillance documented multiple Las Vegas trips between DeBartolo Sr. and the Carabbia brothers (Cleveland family's Youngstown representatives). Jimmy Fratianno (FBI informant, credible track record) confirmed DeBartolo was "very friendly" with Ronald Carabbia and that "they used to go on junkets to the Tropicana." The origin story — DeBartolo sought Carabbia protection after 1950s firebombing campaign against his properties — is internally coherent with how organized crime relationships develop in mob-controlled cities.

**Evidence tier: E2 (FBI surveillance + Fratianno testimony; no criminal transaction proven)**

### Finding 4: Shapiro Murder — Wexner's Attorney, Columbus OC Nexus (E1-E2)

Arthur Shapiro, Wexner's personal attorney at Schwartz Shapiro Kelm & Warren, was murdered on March 6, 1985 — shot twice in the head at a Columbus cemetery, one day before his scheduled grand jury testimony. Columbus Police Department's Organized Crime Bureau concluded the killing bore "strong marks of a La Cosa Nostra 'hit.'" The 1991 Columbus PD analysis placed DeBartolo in an organized crime context alongside Wexner, describing their "well-known history of business and investment partnerships" and characterizing DeBartolo as a "Genovese-LaRocca crime family associate."

**The Suppression:** Columbus Police Chief James G. Jackson ordered the document destroyed in June 1991. When confronted by Channel 4 News, Jackson said "I thought I got rid of it" and called the report "scandalous." The document survived and is available at archive.org.

**Evidence tier: E1 (murder record + document); E2 (organized crime assessment)**
**Significance:** The suppression by the Police Chief himself demonstrates that DeBartolo and Wexner's organized crime connections were known at the executive level of Columbus law enforcement and considered politically too dangerous to pursue.

### Finding 5: The $1.7 Billion Wexner Joint Venture (E2) — NEW FINDING

DeBartolo Sr. and Les Wexner jointly bid $1.7 billion for Carter Hawley Hale Stores (owner of The Broadway, Neiman-Marcus, Bergdorf Goodman, Emporium) in 1984 and again in 1986. The Los Angeles Times (1986) described the bid: "The takeover offer for Carter Hawley brings together the two richest men in Ohio." The bid failed.

This is the most concrete documented expression of the DeBartolo-Wexner business relationship. A coordinated $1.7B joint takeover bid requires intensive mutual trust, shared legal structure, combined financing, and sustained high-level personal collaboration — it is not a casual business interaction.

**Evidence tier: E2 (LA Times contemporaneous press, confirmed via multiple downstream sources)**
**Significance:** Substantially upgrades the DeBartolo-Wexner nexus from "mentioned together in law enforcement file" to documented active business partners.

### Finding 6: Carabbia Brothers — Organized Crime War Navigation (E2)

DeBartolo's documented relationship was with the Carabbia brothers — the Cleveland family's Youngstown representatives. In February 1977 he acquired the Pittsburgh Penguins — placing him simultaneously in the orbit of the Pittsburgh crime family, which was at war with the Carabbias for control of Youngstown. Charlie Carabbia disappeared/was murdered December 13, 1980; DeBartolo's Penguins were Pittsburgh-based. Whether this dual positioning was deliberate hedging or coincidence is unresolved, but the timing is analytically notable.

**Evidence tier: E2**

### Finding 7: Gladio Hypothesis — NEGATIVE (E3, 0.05) (E3)

The operator's original hypothesis — DeBartolo likely involved in US-side Operation Gladio — is assessed at 0.05 confidence after full investigation. DeBartolo shares three structural characteristics with Italian Gladio principals (organized crime, offshore banking, CIA-mob nexus). He is missing the four characteristics required to substantiate the Gladio hypothesis: P2 lodge documentation, CIA operational records, parliamentary confirmation, and any documented connection to NATO stay-behind infrastructure. The US domestic Gladio program is the least documented aspect of Gladio globally. DeBartolo does not appear in any available document connecting him to that program.

**Evidence tier: E3 (structural inference only)**
**Confidence: 0.05**
**Operator note:** The hypothesis was appropriately bounded by the operator's caveat that US domestic Gladio is least documented. This report confirms: the evidence does not currently exist in the public domain to substantiate the hypothesis.

### Finding 8: CIA-Mafia Nexus — INDETERMINATE (E3)

The CIA-Trafficante connection is confirmed at E1 (Church Committee; Operation Mongoose; CIA Family Jewels). DeBartolo's Trafficante association is documented at E2 (DOJ + Customs + Tampa banking nexus). But the chain from Trafficante → DeBartolo → CIA operational asset requires two inferential steps with no primary source support at either. DeBartolo's Metropolitan Bank, Grand Cayman routing, and cash-intensive operations are structurally consistent with CIA off-balance-sheet finance but equally consistent with organized crime money laundering.

**Evidence tier: E3**
**Confidence: 0.10 (CIA operational relationship)**

### Finding 9: The "Lansky Successor" Claim — Single-Source, Unverified (E2/E3)

US Customs Agent Burda's 1981 report characterized DeBartolo's organization in terms suggesting he was positioned as a successor to Meyer Lansky (who was described as "almost senile and getting out of the business"). If true, this would mean DeBartolo's commercial empire was coordinating financial flows for the national crime syndicate — a function of extraordinary scale and significance. However, this claim has not been corroborated by any independent investigation. It remains a single-source Customs assessment (E2/E3) without corroborating primary documentation.

**Evidence tier: E2/E3 (single agency source; not independently verified)**
**Significance:** The most extraordinary claim in the DeBartolo file. Either DeBartolo was the most significant unindicted organized crime financial figure in American history, or the Customs informant who provided this intelligence was exaggerating or wrong.

### Finding 10: DeBartolo Jr. Trump Pardon — Network Continuity (E1)

Trump pardoned Edward DeBartolo Jr. on February 18, 2020 — the same date as Les Wexner's congressional co-conspirator deposition. DeBartolo Jr. had supported Trump's 2016 campaign. Trump's pardon covers DeBartolo Jr.'s 1999 guilty plea ($400K bribe to Louisiana Governor Edwin Edwards). The pardon was characterized by analysts as both a political reward (2016 support) and a strategic gesture toward Youngstown's blue-collar Ohio vote base.

**Evidence tier: E1**
**Note:** The coincidence of the Trump pardon date with the Wexner deposition date is analytically notable but may be coincidental.

## SIGNIFICANCE ASSESSMENT

DeBartolo's significance to the Sherlock PKG-EP investigation is:

1. **Wexner nexus confirmation:** The $1.7B Carter-Hawley-Hale joint venture establishes DeBartolo and Wexner as genuine business partners — not merely proximate figures — substantially enriching the organized crime ecosystem mapping around Wexner.

2. **Genovese family thread:** Both DeBartolo (Genovese-LaRocca Pittsburgh family) and Wexner's logistics partner Walsh (Fat Tony Salerno, Genovese boss) trace to the same national crime family — the Genovese organization. This creates a structural common denominator in the organized crime ecosystem surrounding Wexner.

3. **Shapiro murder context:** DeBartolo's presence in the Shapiro file alongside Wexner, and the subsequent police chief suppression, provides additional context for understanding the extraordinary political protection that appears to have shielded Wexner from organized crime prosecution in Ohio across multiple decades.

4. **Gladio hypothesis — negative finding documented:** The assessed confidence of 0.05 for the Gladio hypothesis is itself significant intelligence. Future researchers can avoid expending resources on this line of inquiry unless new documents emerge.

5. **Metropolitan Bank as potential intelligence nexus:** The Tampa bank operating in Trafficante's city, with CIA-connected Trafficante as the documented organized crime figure linking DeBartolo to the CIA-Mafia nexus, remains the most promising avenue for any future research into intelligence community dimensions.
