## 5. PROMIS SOFTWARE CONNECTION

### 5.1 The INSLAW/PROMIS Background

**Documented Facts (E1):**

- **PROMIS** (Prosecutor's Management Information System) was people-tracking software developed by Inslaw, Inc. in the late 1970s under U.S. Department of Justice grants.
- **1982:** The Department of Justice licensed PROMIS but allegedly refused to pay Inslaw for its proprietary enhancements.
- **1988:** Two separate federal courts ruled that the government "took, converted, stole" PROMIS from Inslaw "through trickery, fraud and deceit."
- **1991:** A court of appeal overturned both rulings on a "jurisdictional technicality" after Justice Department pressure.
- **September 1992:** The House Judiciary Committee released a report on the INSLAW affair. Key findings:
  - The Committee found "serious concerns" that DOJ officials had schemed "to destroy Inslaw and co-opt the rights to its PROMIS software."
  - The report "largely supported the findings of two federal courts" that the DOJ stole the software.
  - High-level DOJ officials knew "INSLAW's claim of PROMIS software ownership was legitimate."
  - Friends of then-AG Edwin Meese, including Reagan associate Dr. Earl Brian, were reportedly allowed to sell pirated PROMIS versions "for their personal financial gain and in support of the intelligence and foreign policy objectives of the United States."

**Evidence Grade:** E1 (Congressional report, court rulings, government records)
