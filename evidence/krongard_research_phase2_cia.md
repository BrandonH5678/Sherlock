## 3. THE SEC-CIA CONSULTATION ON PUT OPTIONS

### 3.1 The Investigation Framework

After September 11, 2001, the SEC initiated what it designated as the "Terrorist Attack Trading Investigation." As part of this investigation, the SEC consulted multiple agencies, including the CIA.

The consultation framework:
- The SEC had identified anomalous pre-9/11 trading activity (documented in Phase 1 report)
- The SEC needed intelligence community assessment of whether the trading patterns indicated foreknowledge
- The CIA was one of several agencies consulted (along with FBI and FinCEN)
- The 9/11 Commission also met with CIA personnel who had "relevant knowledge about the plot financing"

### 3.2 What Was the Nature of the Consultation?

The precise content of the SEC-CIA consultation on the put options has never been publicly disclosed. What is known:

| Known Fact | Source | Evidence Tier |
|------------|--------|---------------|
| The SEC consulted the CIA | 9/11 Commission Report, Staff Statement on Terrorist Financing | E1 |
| The CIA provided input on the trading investigation | 9/11 Commission, SEC official statement | E1 |
| The 9/11 Commission "met with key people from" the CIA "who had relevant knowledge" | 9/11 Commission Report | E1 |
| The ultimate conclusion was "no evidence of foreknowledge" | 9/11 Commission Report | E1 |
| The specific CIA personnel involved in the consultation are not publicly identified | — | GAP |
| The substance of CIA's response is not publicly available | — | GAP |
| Whether Krongard personally participated in or directed the CIA's response is unknown | — | GAP |

### 3.3 Krongard's Position During the Consultation

**The structural conflict of interest:**

Krongard became CIA Executive Director on **March 16, 2001**. The SEC investigation began on **September 12, 2001**. Krongard was therefore the CIA's chief operating officer — the person who managed daily operations, including interagency communications — during the entirety of the SEC's investigation period.

The CIA's response to the SEC inquiry would have been an operational matter. In the normal course of CIA business, the Executive Director's office would have been involved in coordinating any substantive response to another federal agency. Whether Krongard personally handled the SEC consultation, delegated it, or was briefed on it is unknown — but his position meant he had the **authority and institutional access** to shape the CIA's response.

**The conflict:**
- The pre-9/11 put options were executed through **Deutsche Bank Alex. Brown** — the direct successor to the firm Krongard had chaired and run for most of the 1990s
- The trades were routed through Chicago Board Options Exchange accounts associated with Krongard's former institution
- Krongard had left Alex. Brown only ~3.5 years before the trades occurred
- He had personally selected his successor (Mayo Shattuck III) who was running the firm when the trades were executed
- He was now in a position where his office could influence the CIA's assessment of whether those same trades represented foreknowledge

**No evidence of recusal has been found.** This does not prove Krongard failed to recuse — it may be that recusal records exist in classified files or that the issue was handled informally. But the absence of any public evidence of recusal, combined with the obvious conflict, is itself an evidence gap.

Evidence tier: E2 (established facts about positions and timeline); E3 (analytical inference about potential influence)

### 3.4 How CIA's Input Factored Into the Commission's Conclusion

The 9/11 Commission's conclusion that there was "no evidence of foreknowledge" in the put options trading incorporated input from multiple sources: the SEC's own investigation, FBI analysis, FinCEN data, and CIA assessment. The Commission does not attribute its conclusion to any single agency's input.

However, the CIA's assessment would have carried particular weight because:
1. The CIA was the agency best positioned to assess whether foreign intelligence services or terrorist networks might have used financial markets for foreknowledge-based trading
2. The CIA had access to signals intelligence and human intelligence that the SEC did not
3. The CIA's institutional credibility within the national security establishment would have given its assessment significant weight with the Commission

If the CIA told the 9/11 Commission that it found no intelligence indicators connecting the trades to foreknowledge, this assessment would have been difficult for the Commission to override — even if the statistical evidence (Poteshman, Chesney/Crameri/Mancini) showed anomalous trading patterns.

Evidence tier: E3 (analytical reasoning about institutional dynamics; no direct evidence of CIA's specific input)

## 6. OVERALL ASSESSMENT

### 6.1 Analytical Significance of the Krongard-CIA Tenure

Krongard's career trajectory is significant not because it proves a specific conspiracy, but because it represents a **structural pattern** in which:

1. A single individual moved from the **chairmanship of a financial institution** to the **operational leadership of the CIA**
2. That same financial institution subsequently **executed anomalous securities trades** immediately before the most consequential national security event in modern American history
3. That individual was then in a **position of authority** over the agency consulted about whether those trades represented foreknowledge
4. No evidence of **formal recusal** from this conflict of interest has been found
5. The **documentary evidence** that could resolve the question has been destroyed by the SEC
6. The **successor** who ran the financial institution on 9/11 **resigned the day after the attacks** without public explanation

Each of these facts is individually documented. Their combination creates an analytical pattern that warrants continued investigation.

Brown during a period when the firm developed relationships, capabilities, or activities relevant to the pre-9/11 put options; after transitioning to CIA, he was positioned to shape the CIA's assessment of those same options, effectively providing cover for the institution he formerly led.

- In the Sherlock analytical framework, this represents an **open hypothesis with confirmed structural elements and unresolved intentional elements**
