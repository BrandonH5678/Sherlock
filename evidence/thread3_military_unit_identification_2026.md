## II. Missile System: R-12 (8K63) / SS-4 "Sandal"

In October 1982, the 50th Missile Division was armed with **R-12 medium-range ballistic missiles** (NATO: SS-4 "Sandal"):

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| **Type** | Medium-range ballistic missile (MRBM) |
| **Range** | ~2,080 km |
| **Warhead** | Nuclear (single, ~2.3 MT yield) |
| **Targets** | Western European cities and military targets |
| **Deployment at MU 32156** | 1st Bn: 4 pads (1960); 2nd Bn: 4 pads (1960); 3rd Bn: 4 silos (1964) |
| **Total launchers (163rd Regt)** | 12 |
| **Stood down** | 1984 (replaced by SS-20 in 1985) |

**This confirms Knapp's claim: "These missiles were meant to be fired at Western targets, including the U.S."** — partially accurate. R-12 range (~2,080 km) targeted Western Europe, not the continental US. However, from Ukraine the missiles could reach NATO military targets across Western Europe.

### 3.2 The Signal-A System

Soviet RVSN used the **Signal-A multifaceted communications system** to transmit launch orders from the General Staff through the chain: RVSN Main Staff → Armies → **Divisions** → Regiments. At each level, orders were received by specialized equipment called **Baksan**.

The "spontaneous illumination of ALL displays" and appearance of "control code combinations" on the recording tape at division headquarters describes **behavior consistent with the receipt of command signals in the nuclear C2 chain** — signals that would normally only appear during authorized launch procedures or system tests.

### 3.3 MU 32156 "Battery 4" — Confirmed Nuclear Missile Regiment

Witness Kobulyansky at MU 32156 "battery 4" was stationed at the **163rd Missile Regiment** — an operational nuclear missile regiment with 12 R-12 launchers. The term "battery" in witness statements corresponds to a sub-unit (battalion/battery) of this regiment.
