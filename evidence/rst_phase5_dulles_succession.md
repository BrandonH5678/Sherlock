## Executive Summary

PKG-MK established that Allen Dulles managed a unified covert governance portfolio (1953-1961) encompassing MK-Ultra, Gladio stay-behind networks, Operation Mockingbird, foreign coups for Sullivan & Cromwell clients, and political operations — all simultaneously, under one individual's authority. This report asks the successor question: after Kennedy fired Dulles in November 1961, what happened to each element of that portfolio?

**Central finding:** The portfolio did not collapse or fragment randomly. It underwent a three-phase evolution:

1. **Institutional Succession (1961-1973):** Richard Helms inherited operational control of the Directorate of Plans and maintained the core covert action portfolio with remarkable continuity. MK-Ultra continued under Gottlieb. Gladio continued under NATO/CPC structures. Mockingbird media relationships persisted under Cord Meyer and others. Angleton maintained counterintelligence and HTLINGUAL independently. The portfolio remained centralized within CIA institutional channels, even though no single individual held Dulles's unified span of control.

2. **Exposure and Dispersal (1973-1976):** Helms's 1973 destruction of MK-Ultra operational files, Angleton's 1974 firing, the Church Committee (1975), and the post-Watergate political environment shattered the institutional architecture. But the response was not reform — it was dispersal. The Safari Club (1976) created an offshore covert operations capability using allied intelligence services and BCCI banking infrastructure, explicitly designed to circumvent Congressional restrictions. George H.W. Bush as DCI (1976) approved Team B and maintained the Safari Club relationship.

3. **Reconstitution (1981-1987):** William Casey, an OSS veteran with direct lineage to the wartime intelligence establishment, rebuilt the covert operations portfolio under Reagan. Executive Order 12333 (1981) re-expanded CIA authorities. Iran-Contra represented the reconstituted portfolio operating through the NSC rather than CIA channels — the same functions, different institutional vehicle. Casey's daily contact with Roy Cohn connected the reconstituted intelligence portfolio to organized crime, political blackmail, and media manipulation networks.

**The key analytical insight:** The portfolio was never destroyed. It was distributed across a mesh of institutional, personal, and informal channels that proved more resilient than any single point of control. The centralized Dulles model was replaced by a distributed network model — harder to detect, harder to hold accountable, and arguably more durable.

**Confidence in succession framework: 0.65-0.75 (E1/E2 composite)**
The institutional continuities are well-documented (E1). The intentionality of the distributed model is inferential (E2-E3). Whether the distribution was deliberate strategy or emergent adaptation cannot be determined from available evidence.

## Key Findings

### 1. Dulles Portfolio Inventory at Time of Firing (November 1961)

PKG-MK Phase 11 established the unified portfolio at 0.85-0.90 confidence. The complete inventory:

| Portfolio Element | Program/Mechanism | Status at Nov 1961 |
|---|---|---|
| Behavioral control research | MK-Ultra (149 subprojects, 80+ institutions) | Active under Gottlieb/TSS |
| Stay-behind armies | Gladio/NATO CPC (14+ European countries) | Active under NATO structures |
| Media influence | Operation Mockingbird (~400 journalists per Church Committee) | Active under Cord Meyer/Wisner successors |
| Foreign coups | DDP covert action (Iran 1953, Guatemala 1954, Congo, Cuba) | Active — Bay of Pigs failure was catalyst for firing |
| Counterintelligence | CI Staff under Angleton (HTLINGUAL, mole hunts) | Active, semi-autonomous |
| Corporate interface | Sullivan & Cromwell client relationships (United Fruit, Anglo-Iranian Oil/BP) | Personal to Dulles — terminated with his departure |
| Political operations | Domestic political influence, Georgetown Set social network | Personal/informal — continued through social channels |
| Science programs | MK-SEARCH successor planning, OFTEN (1966) | In transition under Gottlieb |
| Financial networks | CIA proprietary companies, front foundations (Human Ecology Fund, Geschickter) | Active under institutional management |

**Critical distinction:** Some portfolio elements were institutional (survived leadership change automatically) and some were personal (required active succession). The institutional elements persisted with minimal disruption. The personal elements — Sullivan & Cromwell corporate nexus, Georgetown Set coordination, political broker functions — are where succession analysis becomes genuinely uncertain.

### 2. Richard Helms as Operational Successor (1962-1973)

Helms was appointed Deputy Director for Plans (DDP) on February 17, 1962 by DCI John McCone, and became DCI himself in 1966. He was the most continuously involved senior CIA officer in the MK portfolio, having directly supervised Gottlieb as DDP.

**What Helms maintained:**
- MK-Ultra/MK-SEARCH operational continuity under Gottlieb (TSS/TSD). Programs continued through the McCone directorship (1961-1965) without apparent disruption. MK-SEARCH formally replaced MK-Ultra in 1964. MK-OFTEN launched in 1966. [E1, 0.90]
- Covert action portfolio: Chile (Track I/Track II against Allende, 1970-1973), Vietnam-era operations, global covert action programs. Helms testified he received direct orders from Nixon on September 15, 1970 to prevent Allende's inauguration. [E1, 0.95]
- Operation CHAOS (1967-1974): domestic surveillance program targeting antiwar movement, compiled files on 7,200 Americans and a 300,000-name index. Authorized by Helms under presidential directive. [E1, 0.95]

**What Helms transformed:**
- The relationship between CIA Director and White House changed fundamentally. Dulles had operated with enormous autonomy under Eisenhower. McCone (1961-1965) and Helms (1966-1973) served presidents (Kennedy, Johnson, Nixon) who demanded closer control. The portfolio elements continued, but the DCI's freedom of independent action diminished. [E2, 0.75]
- Helms navigated the tension between operational continuity and presidential control by compartmentalization — keeping programs running while managing what each president knew. His 1973 perjury before Congress about Chile operations illustrates this strategy. [E1, 0.85]

**The 1973 destruction:** As one of his final acts before leaving the Agency (he was removed by Nixon in February 1973 and sent to Iran as Ambassador), Helms ordered the destruction of all MK-Ultra operational files. Only ~20,000 financial documents survived, having been misfiled in a separate building. The 1963 Inspector General report also survived. This destruction was deliberate and targeted — Helms destroyed the records that would have answered the question of what the MK programs actually did operationally, while leaving enough financial records to claim "transparency." [E1, 0.95]

**Analytical assessment:** Helms was the operational successor, not the strategic successor. He maintained and expanded the covert action portfolio but did not occupy Dulles's unique position as unified portfolio manager with simultaneous control over behavioral research, stay-behind armies, media operations, corporate relationships, and political operations. Under Helms, these functions were maintained but managed through separate institutional channels rather than unified personal direction.

### 3. James Angleton and the Counterintelligence Continuity (1954-1974)

Angleton ran the Counterintelligence Staff from 1954 (appointed by Dulles) until his forced resignation on Christmas Eve 1974 (fired by DCI William Colby after the New York Times exposed his domestic surveillance programs). This 20-year tenure made Angleton the longest-serving portfolio holder in the Dulles succession.

**What Angleton maintained:**
- HTLINGUAL mail opening program (1952-1973): intercepted and opened 215,000 pieces of US mail over two decades. Fell under CI Staff from 1955. [E1, 0.95]
- Mole-hunting operations that paralyzed Soviet operations division for years
- Israeli intelligence liaison (personal relationship, documented) [E2, 0.80]
- Independent operational authority that successive DCIs (McCone, Helms, Schlesinger, Colby) were unable to fully control [E1, 0.85]

**Angleton's semi-autonomous status** is a key succession finding. Dulles appointed him and gave him extraordinary latitude. Subsequent DCIs inherited Angleton but could not effectively supervise him. Colby's decision to fire him in 1974 was partly motivated by Angleton's refusal to submit to normal institutional authority — he operated as a holdover from the Dulles era, maintaining portfolio elements that Dulles had authorized and no successor DCI had the institutional knowledge to fully audit.

**Angleton's Church Committee testimony** was heavily redacted and, by multiple accounts, misleading. Both Angleton and Colby gave the Committee incomplete information about domestic surveillance programs. Angleton's full testimony was not declassified until March 2025. [E1, 0.85]

### 4. The Georgetown Set — Social Network as Coordination Mechanism

The Georgetown Set was an informal social network of OSS veterans, CIA officials, diplomats, journalists, and policymakers who gathered regularly from 1945 through the mid-1970s. Key members included Allen Dulles, Frank Wisner, James Angleton, George Kennan, Dean Acheson, Richard Bissell, Desmond FitzGerald, Joseph Alsop, Stewart Alsop, Tracy Barnes, Thomas Braden, Philip Graham, Cord Meyer, Averell Harriman, John McCloy, and Paul Nitze.

**The Georgetown Set as coordination mechanism:**
- Provided informal policy coordination between CIA, State Department, and media outside of institutional channels [E2, 0.75]
- Joseph Alsop's Sunday "Zoo Parties" (2720 Dumbarton Avenue) functioned as a regular forum where intelligence officials, senators, ambassadors, judges, and journalists debated Cold War strategy [E1, 0.85]
- Philip Graham (Washington Post publisher) was a documented Mockingbird asset; his relationship with the Georgetown Set illustrates how media influence was maintained through social rather than institutional channels [E2, 0.70]
- Cord Meyer, described as Mockingbird's "principal operative," was a core Georgetown Set member [E2, 0.75]

**Succession implications:**
- After Dulles's firing, the Georgetown Set continued functioning as a coordination mechanism for at least another decade. The social network persisted even as institutional control changed. [E2, 0.70]
- Frank Wisner's mental breakdown (September 1958, diagnosed manic depressive, received electroshock therapy) and eventual suicide (1965) removed the operational leader of OPC/DDP covert action, but the social network he helped build survived him. [E1, 0.90]
- The Georgetown Set's decline in the 1970s coincided with the Church Committee exposure, Watergate, and the broader collapse of elite Cold War consensus. The social coordination mechanism degraded at approximately the same time as the institutional architecture. [E2, 0.65]

**Assessment:** The Georgetown Set represents the best-documented example of how informal social networks maintained portfolio coordination after institutional disruption. It was not a conspiracy — it was an elite social circle where people with shared institutional backgrounds, shared worldviews, and overlapping professional responsibilities naturally coordinated their activities. The analytical question is whether this "natural coordination" produced outcomes indistinguishable from deliberate direction. The answer is probably yes, at least partially. [E2/E3, 0.55-0.65]

### 5. George H.W. Bush Trajectory — Intentional Succession or Elite Career Path?

The documented trajectory: Skull and Bones at Yale (1948) → Zapata Petroleum (1953, co-founded with CIA officer Thomas Devine) → CIA Director (1976) → Vice President (1981) → President (1989).

**Documented CIA connections prior to 1976 directorship:**

- **Thomas J. Devine:** Bush's business partner at Zapata Oil was a CIA staff employee who "left" the Agency in 1953 to help establish Zapata. Declassified CIA memo confirms Devine had a "special relationship allowing him to come and go from the agency." From 1963, Devine operated under commercial cover as part of CIA project WUBRINY/LPDICTUM (proprietary commercial operations in Europe). Devine accompanied Bush to Vietnam in December 1967-January 1968, for which Devine was issued an interim top-secret clearance. [E1, 0.85 — declassified CIA memo]

- **Hoover memo (November 23, 1963):** FBI Director Hoover noted a briefing given to "Mr. George Bush of the Central Intelligence Agency" the day after JFK's assassination. Bush denied this referred to him. The CIA initially claimed it referred to a different "George Bush" — a low-level analyst — but this individual later stated under oath that the memo did not refer to him. [E1, 0.80 — FBI memo exists; identification contested]

- **1975 CIA memo:** A November 29, 1975 memo confirms that Bush (then CIA director-designate) had "prior knowledge of the now terminated project WUBRINY/LPDICTUM" through his relationship with Devine. This directly contradicts Bush's claim of no prior CIA involvement. [E1, 0.85]

- **Zapata Oil and Bay of Pigs:** Investigative journalist Russ Baker (Family of Secrets, 2009) documented evidence suggesting Zapata's offshore drilling platforms and ships were used as CIA cover for operations, potentially including Bay of Pigs logistics. The CIA Bay of Pigs operation was code-named "Operation Zapata" and two of the invasion ships were named "Houston" and "Barbara" — circumstantial but notable. [E2/E3, 0.40-0.50]

**As CIA Director (1976):**
- Approved Team B — an exercise that allowed hawkish outsiders (including Paul Wolfowitz) to produce a competitive intelligence assessment that substantially overestimated the Soviet threat. The Senate Select Committee on Intelligence later concluded Team B's composition was biased. Team B veterans became core neoconservative national security establishment figures. [E1, 0.85]
- Announced policy that CIA would not enter "paid or contract relationship" with journalists — but explicitly preserved "voluntary, unpaid cooperation." This was widely assessed as a cosmetic reform. [E1, 0.90]
- Maintained Safari Club relationship (see Finding 7 below). [E2, 0.70]

**Assessment of intentional succession hypothesis:**
- The documented facts — Skull and Bones membership, business partnership with a CIA officer operating under commercial cover, pre-1976 CIA connections, Team B, Safari Club — are individually explainable as features of elite Cold War career paths. Many Ivy League graduates entered intelligence. Oil companies operated in regions of CIA interest.
- However, the cumulative pattern — particularly the Devine relationship and the WUBRINY/LPDICTUM commercial cover project — suggests something beyond coincidental career overlap. Bush's business partner was a documented CIA officer running a CIA commercial cover operation, and Bush denied all prior CIA connections. The denial is more analytically significant than any single piece of evidence.
- **Confidence in intentional succession pathway: 0.45-0.55** — the evidence establishes pre-1976 CIA involvement (E1) but does not establish that the career trajectory was designed as a succession mechanism for the Dulles portfolio specifically. It is more accurately assessed as evidence that Bush was part of the broader intelligence-corporate nexus that Dulles had institutionalized, not necessarily a designated successor.

### 6. William Casey (CACTUS) — OSS-to-Reagan Continuity Node

William Joseph Casey (1913-1987) represents the most direct OSS-to-CIA Director lineage after Dulles himself.

**OSS background:**
- Made Chief of Secret Intelligence (SI) for the European Theater in late December 1944, coordinating deployment of 100+ allied paratrooper teams into occupied Germany
- Operated from London while Dulles ran the Bern station — same OSS network under William Donovan
- Four future DCIs served in the OSS: Allen Dulles, Richard Helms, William Colby, and William Casey [E1, 0.95]

**Reagan's CIA Director (1981-1987):**
- First DCI given Cabinet-level status, elevating CIA to direct policy participation [E1, 0.90]
- Executive Order 12333 (December 4, 1981): re-expanded CIA authorities, extended intelligence community powers, directed federal agencies to cooperate with CIA information requests. Effectively reversed post-Church Committee restrictions. [E1, 0.95]
- Rebuilt covert operations capacity to levels exceeding pre-Church Committee capability [E2, 0.80]
- Iran-Contra: Casey was the Cabinet-level architect of the covert network to resupply the Contras during the Boland Amendment funding cutoff, and promoted secret arms sales to Iran. He approved running these operations through the NSC staff (Oliver North) rather than CIA channels — circumventing the very Congressional oversight the Church Committee had established. [E1, 0.90 — Walsh Iran-Contra Report, Chapter 15]
- Proposed creating a "supersecret covert operation outside the United States and out-of-reach of Congress" funded by Iran weapons sale profits and other sources. This was the most explicit articulation of the distributed, off-the-books model. [E1, 0.85 — documented in Iran-Contra investigation]

**Casey-Cohn channel:**
- PKG-MK/Roy Cohn profile established: Casey "called Roy [Cohn] almost daily" during the 1980 Reagan campaign (switchboard operator Christine Seymour testimony). [E2, 0.75]
- Cohn aided Iran-Contra propaganda apparatus, connecting conservative media to CIA propaganda specialist Walter Raymond Jr.'s Latin America PR campaign. [E2, 0.70]
- Craig Spence connection: Casey reportedly attended a birthday party for Cohn hosted by Spence (Washington Times). [E3, 0.45]

**Assessment:** Casey is the clearest succession node in the portfolio. His OSS lineage connects directly to the wartime intelligence establishment that Dulles helped build. His actions as DCI — rebuilding covert ops, circumventing Congressional oversight, proposing off-the-books operations — represent a conscious reconstitution of the Dulles-era portfolio adapted to a post-Church Committee environment. The key adaptation was institutional: rather than running everything through CIA, Casey distributed operations across the NSC, private networks, and allied intelligence services. [E1/E2, 0.75-0.80]

### 7. The Safari Club and BCCI — Off-the-Books Continuity (1976-1991)

The Safari Club was formed in 1976 as a covert alliance of intelligence services (Saudi Arabia, Egypt, France, Morocco, Iran under the Shah) to conduct operations that the CIA could no longer legally perform after the Church Committee restrictions.

**Key findings:**
- Henry Kissinger is credited with the strategy of supporting the Safari Club implicitly — allowing it to fulfill American objectives by proxy without direct responsibility. [E2, 0.75]
- BCCI (Bank of Credit and Commerce International) served as the financial infrastructure for Safari Club operations. Sheikh Kamal Adham set up the banking network "with the official blessing of the CIA's George Bush" (then-DCI). [E2, 0.65]
- CIA officers Ted Shackley, Thomas Clines, and Edwin Wilson maintained connections with the Safari Club and BCCI even after DCI Stansfield Turner attempted to limit operations under Carter. [E2, 0.70]
- The Safari Club/BCCI model is the clearest example of portfolio dispersal: the same functions (covert operations, arms trafficking, financial manipulation) continued, but through non-CIA institutional channels specifically designed to evade Congressional oversight. [E2, 0.70]

**Succession significance:** The Safari Club represents the moment when the Dulles portfolio model definitively transformed from centralized CIA control to a distributed network model. The functions did not stop. The institutional vehicle changed. This is the structural bridge between the Church Committee exposure (1975) and the Casey reconstitution (1981-1987).

### 8. The Church Committee — Exposure, Fragmentation, or Concealment?

The Church Committee (1975-1976) investigated CIA assassination plots, MK-Ultra, domestic surveillance (CHAOS, COINTELPRO, HTLINGUAL, MINARET, SHAMROCK), and covert action abuses.

**What the Church Committee accomplished:**
- Established permanent Senate Select Committee on Intelligence [E1, 0.95]
- Presidential Executive Order banning political assassinations (Ford's EO 11905, later Reagan's EO 12333) [E1, 0.95]
- FISA (1978) creating judicial oversight of domestic surveillance [E1, 0.95]
- Hughes-Ryan Amendment requiring presidential finding for covert actions [E1, 0.95]
- Public exposure of MK-Ultra (partial — limited by Helms's 1973 records destruction) [E1, 0.90]

**What the Church Committee missed or was denied:**
- CACTUS channel connecting CIA CHAOS to FBI COINTELPRO — never disclosed to the Committee [E2, 0.60-0.70, from PKG-MK Phase 6]
- Full scope of Angleton's operations — both Angleton and Colby gave misleading testimony [E1, 0.85]
- MK-Ultra operational details — destroyed by Helms; Committee worked from ~20,000 surviving financial records and the 1963 IG report [E1, 0.95]
- Gladio stay-behind networks — not exposed until Italian Prime Minister Andreotti's 1990 disclosure [E1, 0.90]
- Safari Club formation (1976) — occurring simultaneously with the Committee's work, designed to circumvent its reforms [E2, 0.70]

**Assessment of Church Committee as portfolio inflection point:**
The Committee caused genuine institutional disruption. Angleton was fired. Helms was convicted of perjury. Programs were formally terminated. Congressional oversight was established. These are real structural changes.

However, the Committee's most significant limitation was temporal: it investigated the past while the intelligence community was simultaneously building the future bypass architecture (Safari Club, BCCI, private networks). The reforms addressed the institutional CIA. The portfolio elements had already begun migrating to non-CIA channels.

**Confidence that Church Committee caused portfolio fragmentation: 0.55-0.65.** The Committee did not destroy the portfolio. It forced the portfolio to evolve from a centralized institutional model to a distributed network model. Whether this evolution was pre-planned or reactive is unknowable from available evidence.

### 9. Roy Cohn as Multi-Domain Continuation Node (from PKG-MK)

PKG-MK and the Cohn Intelligence Profile established Cohn as a documented bridge between organized crime, intelligence agencies, political blackmail, media control, and financial power. His relevance to the Dulles succession:

- **Dulles connection:** Judge David Peck, described as "long-time associate of former CIA Director Allen Dulles," placed Cohn at Saxe, Bacon & O'Shea in 1955. One degree of separation from Dulles at career launch. [E2-E3, 0.50]
- **Casey connection:** Daily phone contact during 1980 campaign, continued through Casey's DCI tenure. Casey used Cohn as a connector to media (Murdoch), political operations (Stone, Manafort), and organized crime networks. [E2, 0.75]
- **Hoover connection:** First-name terms from 1952, FBI protection throughout career. Hoover recommended Cohn's McCarthy appointment. [E1-E2, 0.85]
- **Function:** Cohn maintained the political/legal/blackmail/media portfolio elements that could not be housed within institutional CIA channels — before, during, and after the Church Committee period. His death in 1986 removed a continuation node, but his proteges (Roger Stone, Donald Trump, Rupert Murdoch connections) continued the political operations network. [E2, 0.65]

### 10. Jolyon West as Behavioral Science Continuation Node (from PKG-MK)

PKG-MK Phase 8 documented West as MK-Ultra Subproject 43 researcher (E1, 0.90) who appeared at Ruby (1964), Haight-Ashbury/Manson milieu (1966-67), Hearst (1975-76), Sirhan Sirhan, and McVeigh (1995). His relevance to succession:

- West's operational pattern continued for 30+ years after Dulles's departure, suggesting the behavioral science portfolio element was maintained through individual researcher continuity rather than institutional channels. [E2, 0.50-0.60]
- Between 1974 and 1989, West received at least $5,110,099 in federal grants through NIMH — a documented CIA funding conduit. Federal grant funding replaced direct CIA funding after Church Committee exposure. [E1, 0.80]
- West's dual-function operative model (legitimate academic + intelligence-adjacent activity) is the behavioral science equivalent of the Safari Club model: same functions, different institutional vehicle. [E2/E3, 0.50-0.60]

### 11. The Centralized-to-Distributed Evolution Model

**Phase A — Centralized (1953-1961):** Dulles personally managed unified portfolio. One individual, one institutional base (CIA DCI), direct authority over all elements.

**Phase B — Institutional Continuity (1961-1973):** Helms maintained operational portfolio through CIA institutional channels. McCone as DCI was a placeholder (no intelligence background). Angleton maintained CI portfolio semi-autonomously. Georgetown Set maintained informal coordination. Portfolio elements continued but without unified personal direction.

**Phase C — Forced Dispersal (1973-1980):** Helms destruction (1973), Angleton firing (1974), Church Committee (1975), Carter-era restrictions created institutional crisis. Response: Safari Club, BCCI, private networks. Portfolio elements migrated from institutional CIA to distributed non-governmental channels.

**Phase D — Reconstitution (1981-1987):** Casey rebuilt covert operations through EO 12333 expansion, Iran-Contra NSC channel, and off-the-books proposals. The reconstituted portfolio was deliberately distributed across CIA, NSC, private networks, and allied intelligence services. The lesson learned was that centralization creates vulnerability to exposure.

**Phase E — Permanent Distribution (1987-present):** Casey's death (May 1987), Iran-Contra exposure, and BCCI collapse (1991) forced further adaptation. The post-Casey model permanently distributed portfolio elements across multiple institutional and non-institutional channels with no single coordination point.

**The evolution is from tree to mesh:** Dulles sat at the root of a tree structure. The post-Church Committee architecture is a mesh network with multiple redundant pathways and no single point of failure — or accountability.

## Negative Findings

1. **No evidence of a formal "succession plan" from Dulles to any individual.** The portfolio dispersal appears emergent/adaptive rather than pre-planned. Multiple institutional and personal succession pathways operated independently. [Searched for: Dulles correspondence designating successors, institutional transition plans. Found: none.]

2. **No documented direct Dulles-Bush relationship.** Despite Bush's documented pre-1976 CIA connections, no direct communication between Dulles and Bush has been found. The Skull and Bones connection (both members) is institutionally suggestive but not operationally documented. [E4 — absence of evidence in a domain where documentation should exist for a direct operational relationship.]

3. **Sullivan & Cromwell corporate interface did NOT transfer.** Dulles's unique role as simultaneous CIA Director and former Sullivan & Cromwell partner — using CIA covert action to serve former firm clients (United Fruit, Anglo-Iranian Oil) — was personal to Dulles. No subsequent DCI had this dual corporate-intelligence position. This portfolio element was terminated, not transferred. [E1, 0.85]

4. **No evidence that the Georgetown Set evolved into a formal coordination body.** It remained an informal social network that declined naturally in the 1970s. Claims of continued formal coordination after the mid-1970s are unsupported. [E2, 0.75 negative]

5. **George H.W. Bush's Zapata Oil-Bay of Pigs connection remains circumstantial.** Operation name ("Zapata"), ship names ("Houston," "Barbara"), and Devine's CIA role are suggestive but do not constitute direct evidence of Zapata company involvement in the operation. [E2/E3, 0.40-0.50 — insufficient for analytical confidence]

6. **Casey's death (May 6, 1987) — no identified successor as portfolio coordinator.** Casey died of a brain tumor before Iran-Contra testimony. No subsequent DCI attempted the same comprehensive portfolio reconstitution. The distributed model became permanent default, not by design but by the absence of another Casey-type figure. [E2, 0.65]

7. **Angleton-Cohn direct relationship: NOT FOUND.** Despite both operating in overlapping domains (counterintelligence, political operations, blackmail), no documented direct relationship between Angleton and Cohn has been identified. Cohn's IC connections ran through Hoover (FBI) and Casey (CIA), not CI Staff. [From Roy Cohn Intelligence Profile — confirmed gap]

8. **No evidence that Gladio stay-behind networks were affected by Dulles's departure.** These operated under NATO/CPC institutional structures and continued without apparent disruption through multiple DCI changes until exposure in 1990. The portfolio element was institutional, not personal. [E1, 0.85]
