## ANALYST NOTE

Edward J. DeBartolo Sr. died on December 19, 1994, having never been criminally charged with any organized crime offense. However, at least four separate law enforcement agencies — the US Department of Justice, the US Customs Service, the FBI, and the Pennsylvania Crime Commission — generated documents characterizing him as an associate of major organized crime figures. The 1991 Columbus Police Department homicide investigation also identified him in an organized crime nexus. This file documents what those agencies found, the evidentiary basis for their assessments, and the broader criminal ecosystem in which DeBartolo operated.

**Critical methodological note:** DeBartolo's denials were consistent and his attorneys aggressively contested organized crime characterizations. He was never convicted or indicted. The evidence assembled here reflects law enforcement assessments, not proven criminal conduct. Each claim is tier-graded accordingly.

## 1. DOJ ORGANIZED CRIME PRINCIPAL SUBJECTS LIST (1970)

### What the List Is

The DOJ's "Organized Crime Principal Subjects List" (also termed the "Organized Crime Index" in some sources) was compiled beginning in the late 1960s and formally distributed to multiple government agencies in 1971. The list catalogued individuals who appeared in investigative reports as suspected of having links to organized crime. Critically: **inclusion on the list did not require that the subject be the target of a criminal investigation, nor did it require criminal charges.** An individual's name appearing in any investigative report — as an associate, a business contact, a named figure in wiretap transcripts — could trigger listing. [E2: multiple sources citing DOJ criteria]

| Aspect | Detail | Evidence Tier |
|--------|--------|---------------|
| DeBartolo listed | Yes — confirmed by multiple independent sources | E2 (encyclopedia.com, grokipedia, south side sox, Wikipedia) |
| Year of listing | 1970 memo; list distributed 1971 | E2 |
| Basis for listing | Listed as associate of Santos Trafficante Jr., alongside Lansky and Marcello in 1970 DOJ memo | E2 |
| DeBartolo's response | Challenged listing through attorney Charles F.C. Ruff when seeking Oklahoma racetrack license | E2 (encyclopedia.com) |
| Outcome of challenge | Not removed from list; DeBartolo still obtained Oklahoma license | E2 |
| List criteria | Names appearing in investigative reports; no criminal charges required | E2 |

The listing is significant not as proof of criminal activity but as proof that federal investigators had placed DeBartolo in documented proximity to three of the most consequential organized crime figures in mid-20th century America — Trafficante, Lansky, and Marcello — sufficiently documented that his name entered the federal catalog alongside theirs.

### The 1970 DOJ Memo: Trafficante, Lansky, Marcello

The specific triggering document appears to be a 1970 US Justice Department memorandum that named DeBartolo as an "associate of Florida crime boss Santos Trafficante Jr., alongside Meyer Lansky and Carlos Marcello." [E2: grokipedia, multiple secondary sources]. The original memo has not been reproduced in publicly available sources examined for this report, meaning the description of its contents is E2 (corroborated secondary journalism) rather than E1 (direct document citation).

## 2. PENNSYLVANIA CRIME COMMISSION ASSESSMENT

### The Assessment

The Pennsylvania Crime Commission, an independent state law enforcement body established to investigate organized crime, characterized DeBartolo as an **"associate of the Genovese-LaRocca crime family in Pittsburgh."** This characterization appears in the Columbus Police Department's 1991 "Shapiro Homicide Investigation: Analysis and Hypothesis" report, which drew on Pennsylvania Crime Commission findings. [E2: Unlimited Hangout / archive.org Shapiro file]

| Aspect | Detail | Evidence Tier |
|--------|--------|---------------|
| Characterization | "Associate of the Genovese-LaRocca crime family in Pittsburgh" | E2 (Columbus PD citing Penn Crime Commission) |
| Source document | Shapiro Homicide Investigation: Analysis and Hypothesis (Columbus Police, June 1991) | E2 (available at archive.org) |
| Secondary source | Pennsylvania Crime Commission reports (cited by Columbus PD) | E2 |
| Suppression | Columbus Police Chief James G. Jackson ordered the document destroyed in June 1991, same month it was completed; when confronted by Channel 4 News, Jackson said "I thought I got rid of it" and termed the report "scandalous" | E2 (Unlimited Hangout / freepress.org) |
| Status | Report survived suppression; available on Internet Archive | E1-accessible (digitized original) |

### The Genovese-LaRocca Family: Who They Were

The Pittsburgh crime family — commonly called the "LaRocca family" — was simultaneously affiliated with and semi-autonomous from the Genovese family of New York. The Pittsburgh family operated under John Sebastian LaRocca (May 1, 1901 – December 3, 1984), who served as boss from 1956 until his death.

| LaRocca Family Fact | Detail | Evidence Tier |
|--------------------|--------|---------------|
| John LaRocca | Born Sicily; boss from 1956 to 1984 death | E1 (court records, Wikipedia) |
| Predecessor | Frank Amato (stepped down 1956 due to kidney disease) | E2 (Wikipedia) |
| Territory | Pittsburgh; southwestern Pennsylvania; expanded into Ohio | E1 (court records) |
| Youngstown connection | LaRocca established Ohio rackets, sharing income with Cleveland family | E2 (Wikipedia) |
| National alliances | Close working relationships with Carlo Gambino (NY), Angelo Bruno (Philadelphia), Russell Bufalino (Pittston), Nick Civella (Kansas City), **Santo Trafficante Jr. (Tampa)** | E2 (Wikipedia, mafiahistory.us) |
| Panel system (1978) | Aging LaRocca established panel: Michael Genovese, Gabriel Mannarino, Joseph Pecora (WV) | E2 (Wikipedia) |
| Youngstown subterritory | Mannarino controlled Westmoreland County and Youngstown, Ohio; inherited by Ciancutti and Naples | E2 (Wikipedia) |

**The Genovese connection** is structural: the Pittsburgh family's "Genovese-LaRocca" designation reflected both its organizational lineage (Pittsburgh originated as an extension of Genovese NY interests) and its ongoing cooperative relationship with the Genovese family in New York. DeBartolo's characterization as a "Genovese-LaRocca associate" placed him specifically within the Pittsburgh-based network that controlled organized crime in the Youngstown area — consistent with his documented Carabbia family relationship (see Section 4).

### DeBartolo's Specific Relationship with LaRocca

No direct documentary evidence of a personal DeBartolo-LaRocca meeting or specific transaction has been identified in open-source research. The Pennsylvania Crime Commission's "associate" designation is likely derived from DeBartolo's documented relationship with the Carabbia brothers (Cleveland family's Youngstown representatives) and the Pittsburgh family's parallel presence in Youngstown, rather than from a direct LaRocca-DeBartolo business relationship. "Associate" in organized crime taxonomy does not require direct contact with the boss — it denotes an individual operating within the family's sphere who provides services, receives protection, or maintains business relationships with family members. [E2: methodology inference from standard OC taxonomy]

### Agent Burda: What We Know

William F. Burda was a US Customs Service special agent, presumably based in Florida given the geographic focus of his reporting. His reports on DeBartolo appear to have been generated from confidential source intelligence (informant reporting) rather than direct surveillance or documentary evidence. The reports surfaced publicly during DeBartolo's Oklahoma racetrack licensing proceedings in 1985, when rival applicant Tom Russell used them to challenge DeBartolo's fitness. [E2: grokipedia]

The Burda reports have not been independently verified against original Customs Service files in open-source research. The FDLE (Florida Department of Law Enforcement) separately investigated Metropolitan Bank and found anomalies but concluded: "We didn't find any evidence of narcotics laundering per se, but what we did find shocked us." The FDLE special agent's congressional testimony (1984) suggests there was financial irregularity without proven narcotics trafficking. [E2: Tampa Bay Times]

The Grand Cayman routing is architecturally significant: Grand Cayman-based financial structures were the standard mechanism for offshore money laundering in this era (1975-1982), used by drug cartels, intelligence operations, and organized crime alike. The WFC Corporation-Grand Cayman-Metropolitan Bank chain, as alleged by Burda, is consistent with standard offshore laundering methodology. However, the FDLE's inability to prove narcotics laundering means this remains E2 (alleged, not proven).

## 4. FBI WIRETAP EVIDENCE AND THE FRATIANNO TESTIMONY

### Jimmy Fratianno: Who Was He?

Aladena "Jimmy the Weasel" Fratianno (1913–1993) was a Los Angeles-based mob figure and hitman who became one of the FBI's most significant organized crime informants after turning state's evidence in the late 1970s. He was the acting boss of the Los Angeles crime family for a period. His cooperation resulted in multiple convictions of organized crime figures. His testimony is E2 — credible informant with corroborated track record, but self-interested and unverified against independent documentation in all instances.

### Fratianno's Statements on DeBartolo

| Statement | Detail | Evidence Tier |
|-----------|--------|---------------|
| "Very friendly" | Fratianno told a Moldea associate that DeBartolo Sr. was "very friendly" with Ronald Carabbia | E2 (Dan Moldea, "Interference"; multiple secondary sources) |
| Las Vegas junkets | "They used to go on junkets to the Tropicana [in Las Vegas]" | E2 (Moldea/secondary sources) |
| Gambling behavior | DeBartolo was "a pretty heavy gambler. He would lose a lot of money. He had a pretty good line of credit in Vegas." | E2 (Moldea) |
| FBI wiretap context | A separate FBI wiretap picked up Fratianno discussing arranging a meeting with New Mexico criminal lawyer William Marchiondo via Youngstown figure Ronald Carabbia, connected to DeBartolo's 49ers purchase | E2 (Moldea, "Interference") |
| FBI surveillance | FBI agents followed DeBartolo Sr. and the Carabbias on multiple Las Vegas trips, documented gambling sprees | E2 (FBI dossier 1985, cited by Moldea) |

**Source quality note:** Dan Moldea's 1989 book "Interference: How Organized Crime Influences Professional Football" (William Morrow & Company) is the primary aggregating source for Fratianno's DeBartolo statements. ### The Firebombing Origin Story

According to FBI records cited by Dan Moldea, DeBartolo Sr.'s relationship with the Carabbia brothers originated when rival Cleveland mob elements launched a firebombing campaign against DeBartolo's shopping center storefronts in the early 1950s. DeBartolo turned to the Carabbias for protection, establishing the relationship that FBI surveillance later documented through Las Vegas junkets and social contacts. [E2: FBI 1985 dossier / Moldea]

This origin story is analytically important: it presents DeBartolo's organized crime relationship as initially defensive (seeking protection as a victim of extortion) rather than as an active criminal partnership. This framing is consistent with how organized crime relationships frequently develop — legitimate businessmen in mob-controlled cities often had to purchase protection from the same networks preying on them. The question that cannot be resolved from open-source materials is whether DeBartolo's relationship evolved beyond protection-purchasing into active financial partnership.

4. Social relationships with Trafficante, Marcello network figures

### DeBartolo's Navigation of the Carabbia-Naples Divide

DeBartolo Sr.'s documented relationship was with the Carabbia brothers — the Cleveland family's Youngstown representatives. When the Pittsburgh family under Naples and Prato eliminated the Carabbias and took control of Youngstown around 1980, DeBartolo's protection arrangement would have required renegotiation. Charlie Carabbia disappeared December 13, 1980. DeBartolo Sr. acquired the Pittsburgh Penguins in February 1977 — placing him in the Pittsburgh family's orbit simultaneously with his Carabbia connections in Youngstown. Whether this dual relationship was deliberate hedging or coincidence is unresolved. [E2: Wikipedia / ganglandwire.com]

## ANALYTICAL SUMMARY

### What the Evidence Establishes

1. **Multiple federal and state law enforcement agencies independently characterized DeBartolo as an organized crime associate** — DOJ, FBI, US Customs, Pennsylvania Crime Commission, and Columbus PD all placed him in organized crime proximity. This convergence across agencies that do not share files in real-time is the strongest indicator of legitimate law enforcement concern rather than a single agency's error.

2. **The Carabbia relationship is the most solidly documented** — FBI surveillance documented Las Vegas trips with the Carabbia brothers; Fratianno's testimony (E2) is consistent with the surveillance; the firebombing-protection origin story is internally coherent. This is not allegation — it is documented association.

3. **The Trafficante-Lansky-Marcello naming is law enforcement assessment, not proven transaction** — the DOJ memo and Customs reports reflect intelligence-derived assessments rather than prosecuted facts. They represent what multiple agencies believed to be true, not what was proven in court.

4. **Metropolitan Bank is the most documentable financial anomaly** — a Grand Cayman-routed loan structure through a DeBartolo-controlled Florida bank, combined with 40% loan delinquency, in Trafficante's home city, is structurally consistent with a money movement operation. The FDLE's "shocked" finding suggests significant irregularity even without prosecutable narcotics evidence.

5. **The Shapiro file suppression demonstrates official awareness and deliberate concealment** — a Columbus Police Chief ordering the destruction of an official investigative document linking Ohio's two wealthiest men (DeBartolo, Wexner) to organized crime is not routine administrative error. It is evidence that the organized crime connections were known at the executive level of Ohio law enforcement and considered politically too dangerous to pursue.

### What Remains Unestablished

- No proven criminal transaction between DeBartolo and any organized crime figure
- No specific Teamsters pension fund loans to DeBartolo properties confirmed
- No documented direct meeting with Trafficante, Lansky, or Marcello
- The "Lansky successor" claim remains a single-source Customs allegation without corroboration
- DeBartolo's specific organizational role (if any) within the Genovese-LaRocca family remains undefined

### Sherlock Intelligence Grade

**Overall assessment: DeBartolo Sr. was a substantial-wealth businessman whose empire was embedded within organized crime networks that provided protection, likely financial services, and political facilitation across multiple decades.** The weight of law enforcement documentation is too consistent and multi-agency to dismiss as smear campaign artifacts. However, the specific claims about Lansky succession and narcotics laundering remain insufficiently proven to state as fact. He operated at the nexus of legitimate business and organized crime in a pattern typical of major legitimate businessmen in mob-controlled Rust Belt cities — not running crime, but not independent of it either.
