## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Alan "Ace" Greenberg (1927–2014) was the chief executive of Bear Stearns from 1978 to 1993 and Chairman of its Executive Committee from 1993 to the firm's 2008 collapse. He was simultaneously Jeffrey Epstein's original institutional patron, Roy Cohn's social acquaintance, and the CEO who ran Bear Stearns during its tenure as a BCCI Treasury broker — a relationship confirmed at E1 by the Price Waterhouse Sandstorm Report (1991). He personally maintained a 27-year relationship with Epstein after Epstein left Bear Stearns in 1981, culminating in the Liquid Funding Ltd. vehicle ($6.7B, Epstein chairman, Bear Stearns 40%), which was paid off in anomalous circumstances three weeks after the firm's collapse in 2008.

**The synthesis finding:** Greenberg was **not demonstrably a CIA asset**, but was a foundational institutional enabler whose decisions — hiring Epstein (1976), maintaining Epstein as a client (1981–2008), serving as a BCCI Treasury broker (1983–1986), co-owning Liquid Funding Ltd. (2000–2008) — consistently placed Bear Stearns at the intersection of government finance, intelligence-adjacent banking, and offshore financial structures. Whether this pattern reflects deliberate intelligence coordination or the natural position of a major primary dealer whose CEO maintained relationships with intelligence-adjacent individuals cannot be determined from available evidence.

**The Greenberg Bridge Node hypothesis is CONFIRMED at E2:** Greenberg is the structural hinge connecting the Cohn organized crime/political blackmail network (social intersection, 1983), the Epstein trafficking/intelligence network (institutional patron, 1976–2008), and the BCCI intelligence banking network (institutional broker, 1983–1986). The connections are documented. The deliberateness of the intersection is unproven.

## 2. THE FOUR ANOMALIES — SYNTHESIS ASSESSMENT

### 2.1 Anomaly One: The Military Gap (1927–1949)

Greenberg was born November 3, 1927. He graduated from the University of Missouri in 1949 and arrived at Bear Stearns the same year. He was 17–18 at the time of the D-Day landings (1944) and 19–20 when Japan surrendered (1945). During the Korean War, he would have been 22–26 (1950–1953).

| Question | Assessment | Evidence Tier |
|----------|------------|---------------|
| Did Greenberg serve in WWII? | Not documented. No service record has been cited in any biography. Given his age (17 at war's end) he likely received a late-war draft notice at most. | UNVERIFIED |
| Did Greenberg serve in Korea? | Not documented. He would have been prime draft age (22–26). Absence of service documentation for a public figure is anomalous but not conclusive without FOIA of selective service records. | UNVERIFIED |
| Does the gap suggest intelligence service? | SPECULATION. No evidence. Military service absence in this era sometimes reflects medical disqualification, essential civilian occupation deferment, or simply late enrollment. Without FOIA, the gap cannot be characterized. | E3 (0.10) |

**Synthesis verdict:** The military gap is the most unresolved biographical anomaly in the Greenberg file. It does not support the CIA hypothesis in the absence of specific evidence.

### 2.2 Anomaly Two: Epstein Hire and Retention

The hire of Jeffrey Epstein (1976) with a fraudulent resume has been explained via two competing narratives:
- **Narrative A (family relationship):** Epstein tutored Greenberg's son Ted and was simultaneously dating Greenberg's daughter Lynne. The "Dalton parent" referral was internal to the Greenberg family.
- **Narrative B (intelligence referral):** Epstein was sent to Bear Stearns via a Roy Cohn/CIA channel.

| Assessment | Evidence | Confidence |
|------------|----------|------------|
| Narrative A: Greenberg family connection | E2 — multiple corroborated accounts | 0.80 |
| Narrative B: Intelligence referral | E3 — no evidence; Cohn-Greenberg documented only from 1983, seven years after hire; no evidence of Cohn-Epstein link before Bear Stearns | 0.10 |

**Synthesis verdict:** The family relationship explains the hire with near-complete parsimony. Resume fraud discovery but retention (because Epstein was dating the CEO's daughter) follows the human logic of 1970s Wall Street. The intelligence referral hypothesis requires evidence that does not exist.
