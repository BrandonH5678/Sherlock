## Executive Summary

This audit identifies 22 crisis junctures across the PKG-RESTRUCTURE, PKG-MK, and PKG-XFIN evidence base and scores each for two competing interpretive models:

- **Foraging (exploitation):** Positioned actors opportunistically harvest gains from crises they did not create. The crisis is exogenous; the benefit is captured through structural position. Predicts 40-60% amplification decisions (the rest being genuine stabilization or neutral exploitation).
- **Wildcrafting (cultivation):** Positioned actors actively shape, prolong, or worsen crises to maximize extractable gains. The crisis may be exogenous in origin, but its scale, duration, or aftermath is deliberately amplified. Predicts >75% amplification decisions.

Two additional models are scored where relevant:
- **Farming (institutional design):** Actors design post-crisis institutions to generate recurring yields from stable systems, not from crisis itself.
- **Breeding (selection):** Actors select and elevate successor operators who will maintain the extractive architecture.

**Key diagnostic finding:** Of 22 junctures examined, **17 show amplification decisions** (77%) — defined as cases where a stabilization alternative existed, was feasible, and was not chosen, with the decision benefiting the decision-maker. This percentage falls within the wildcrafting prediction range (>75%) and exceeds the foraging prediction (40-60%).

**However:** The amplification is not uniform. It clusters heavily in **post-crisis architecture design** (Phases 2-4, 7) rather than in **crisis initiation** (Phases 1-2). The evidence does NOT support crisis *engineering* (no E1 evidence for deliberate initiation of WWI, WWII, Russian Revolution, or Soviet collapse). It DOES support crisis *prolongation* and *exploitation of aftermath* at E1 level across multiple phases. The system amplifies crises it did not start — which is wildcrafting behavior, not farming or breeding.

**Revised model assessment:** The data is most consistent with a **foraging-wildcrafting hybrid** — actors forage during the crisis itself (opportunistic positioning) and wildcraft during the aftermath (active shaping of post-crisis architecture to maximize long-term extraction). Pure foraging cannot account for the 77% amplification rate. Pure wildcrafting overstates the evidence for crisis initiation. The hybrid model fits the actual evidence pattern.

### What the Actual Percentage Indicates

The 77% amplification rate is diagnostic. It exceeds what pure opportunism (foraging) would predict. If actors were simply harvesting gains from crises they did not shape, we would expect roughly half the post-crisis junctures to feature genuine stabilization choices — because stabilization also generates profits (reconstruction contracts, new market access, institutional legitimacy). The fact that amplification was chosen at 77% of junctures, across 109 years, multiple continents, and diverse institutional contexts, indicates a **structural preference for amplification over stabilization**.

This does NOT require a conspiracy. It requires:
1. **Structural position** — the same actors are positioned to design post-crisis architecture
2. **Incentive alignment** — amplification generates more extractable value than stabilization
3. **Accountability absence** — the architecture documented in PKG-RESTRUCTURE Phase 5 (Church Committee circumvention, record destruction, cosmetic reform) ensures amplification decisions face no consequences

The system selects for amplification the way evolution selects for fitness — not through conscious direction but through differential survival of strategies that maximize extraction.

## Key Findings

### KF-1: The Amplification Rate Exceeds Foraging Predictions (E2, 0.75)
77% of crisis junctures feature amplification decisions. Foraging (pure exploitation) predicts 40-60%. The excess amplification (17-37 percentage points above foraging baseline) is attributable to active shaping of post-crisis architecture and deliberate circumvention of accountability mechanisms. This is consistent with wildcrafting — cultivation of crisis conditions to maximize extractable value.

### KF-2: Amplification Clusters in Aftermath, Not Initiation (E1-E2, 0.85)
The strongest evidence for amplification is in post-crisis architecture design (9 of 22 junctures), not crisis initiation. No E1 evidence supports deliberate engineering of WWI, WWII, Russian Revolution, or Soviet collapse. The pattern is: exogenous crisis (or crisis of unclear origin) → positioned actors shape the aftermath to maximize long-term extraction. This is the core wildcrafting signature — you did not plant the forest, but you actively shaped what grows in it.

### KF-3: The Accountability-Avoidance Architecture Is Itself an Amplification Mechanism (E1, 0.80)
Five junctures (#13, 14, 15, 19, 20) involve the active circumvention of reforms designed to prevent future amplification. The Safari Club was formed simultaneously with the Church Committee. Casey's EO 12333 reversed Church Committee restrictions. Bush's Mockingbird "reform" preserved the capability. Senate inaction on The Seed enabled Straight Inc. These are not passive failures — they are active decisions to preserve amplification capacity. The accountability-avoidance architecture functions as a meta-amplification mechanism: it amplifies the system's capacity to amplify future crises.

### KF-4: Cross-Faction Operations During Active Conflict Are the Strongest Wildcrafting Evidence (E1, 0.85)
Standard Oil's 1939 agreement to work with IG Farben "whether or not the U.S. came in," Ford's simultaneous production for both sides, BIS processing looted gold while Allied central bankers sat on its board, and Vickers using Krupp fuzes to kill German soldiers — these represent active decisions to maintain extractive operations across active conflict lines. The stabilization alternative in each case was straightforward: stop. The decision to continue was not foraging (opportunistic harvesting) but wildcrafting (active cultivation of conditions that maximize extraction regardless of which side prevails).

### KF-5: The Post-Soviet Episode Is the Clearest Modern Wildcrafting Case (E1, 0.88)
Three junctures (#16, 17, 18) — voucher privatization, loans-for-shares, and the 1996 IMF political loan — demonstrate conscious amplification decisions with documented alternatives. Sachs requested a $15B stabilization fund (rejected). Speed-over-equity was a "conscious policy choice" (acknowledged by architects). Loans-for-shares auctions were rigged by design. The IMF loan was "all too obviously a political decision" (IMF officials). In each case, a stabilization alternative was available, was identified at the time, and was rejected in favor of amplification. Shleifer/Hay's court-documented self-dealing proves that at minimum some Western actors knew they were amplifying for personal benefit.

### KF-6: The Foraging-Wildcrafting Hybrid Model Best Fits the Evidence (E3, 0.70)
Pure foraging (40-60% amplification) underpredicts. Pure wildcrafting (>75%) correctly predicts the rate but overstates crisis initiation. The actual pattern is a two-phase cycle: **Phase 1 (Foraging)** — positioned actors exploit crises of exogenous or unclear origin, harvesting immediate gains through structural position. **Phase 2 (Wildcrafting)** — the same actors actively shape post-crisis architecture, design institutions, circumvent accountability, and calibrate enforcement to maximize long-term extraction. The transition from Phase 1 to Phase 2 is where foraging becomes wildcrafting: the decision to design Dawes Plan circular flows, to preserve the BIS, to create unvouchered CIA funds, to run rigged privatization auctions — these are not passive exploitation. They are active cultivation.
