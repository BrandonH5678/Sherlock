## Executive Summary

World War I produced one of the largest transfers of financial power in modern history: the United States shifted from the world's largest debtor nation to its largest creditor, New York displaced London as the global financial capital, and a small number of banking and industrial firms extracted extraordinary profits from the conflict. The Federal Reserve System, established in December 1913 — just months before the war began — provided the institutional mechanism through which $25.6 billion in war bonds were sold to the American public, effectively socializing the cost of Allied financing that had originally been arranged by private banks, principally J.P. Morgan & Co.

The question of whether financial elites deliberately engineered or prolonged the war for profit must be assessed with discipline. The Nye Committee (1934-1936) found "ample evidence" of excessive profiteering but "little support for the theory that the industry had conspired to draw the nation into war." The structural reality is more subtle: Morgan's $3 billion in Allied purchasing contracts created a financial entanglement so deep that by 1917, US entry into the war became the only way to guarantee repayment of private loans. Whether this was deliberate strategy or emergent consequence is the central analytical question.

The most documented cross-faction connection is the Warburg family: Paul Warburg served as Federal Reserve Vice Chairman (1916-1918) while his brother Max Warburg advised Kaiser Wilhelm II and served as financial adviser to the German chancellor. This is not conspiratorial speculation — it is biographical fact recorded in Federal Reserve official histories and German parliamentary records. Its *meaning*, however, is genuinely disputed.

## Key Findings

### Finding 1: J.P. Morgan as Allied Commercial Agent — Scale and Implications
**Evidence Tier:** E1
**Confidence:** 0.95

J.P. Morgan Jr. was designated as Britain's sole purchasing agent in the United States in January 1915. Between January 1915 and April 1917 (US entry into the war), Morgan became the world's largest single purchaser of goods, procuring more than **$3 billion** (in 1913 dollars) in war-related materials for the Allies, with 60% of that total going toward arms and munitions. The firm earned a 1% commission on these transactions — approximately **$30 million** in direct fees.

Beyond purchasing, Morgan underwrote **$1.5 billion** in war loans to London over the course of the war. On September 28, 1915, a Morgan-led syndicate of approximately 2,200 banks floated the **Anglo-French Loan of $500 million** — at that point the largest single loan in financial history.

The conflict of interest was structural: by 1917, Morgan had so much capital tied to Allied victory that a Central Powers win would have been catastrophic for the firm. The Nye Committee found that "bankers had pressured Wilson to intervene in the war in order to protect their loans abroad." By spring 1917, Britain and France had exhausted their credit lines. The US government either had to guarantee repayment of Allied loans or shipments of war materiel would cease, meaning Allied defeat. US entry into the war effectively socialized Morgan's private risk onto the American taxpayer.

**Sources:** 1914-1918-Online Encyclopedia (House of Morgan entry); Anglo-French Financial Commission (Wikipedia, citing primary financial records); PBS American Experience; Doughboy Foundation (citing Nye Committee records)

### Finding 2: The Warburg Dual Position — Documented Fact, Disputed Meaning
**Evidence Tier:** E1 (biographical facts); E3 (intentional coordination claim)
**Confidence:** 0.90 (dual position existed); 0.20 (coordinated strategy)

**Paul Warburg** (1868-1932): German-born, naturalized US citizen (1911). One of the principal architects of the Federal Reserve System (1913). Served as one of the original Federal Reserve Board members (1914) and as Vice Chairman (1916-1918). Resigned when the US entered the war against Germany — "a blow from which Warburg never recovered" (Federal Reserve History).

**Max Warburg** (1867-1946): Head of M.M. Warburg & Co., Hamburg (1910-1938). Advised Kaiser Wilhelm II prior to WWI. In September 1907, he gave a speech at the German Bankers' Association on "Financial Preparedness for War." During WWI, he was one of the leading figures advising German politicians, diplomats, and the military on financial matters. In October 1918, he was appointed financial adviser to Chancellor Prinz Max von Baden.

A Cambridge University Press academic study ("Networks and Financial War: The Brothers Warburg in the First Age of Globalization") documents that the brothers were "on both sides of the Atlantic energetically pushing for German-American institutions."

**Analytical assessment:** The dual position is indisputable fact (E1). The conspiratorial interpretation — that the brothers coordinated a deliberate strategy to profit from both sides — has no documentary support (E3 at best, E4 for stronger claims). The more parsimonious explanation is that a transnational banking family found itself divided by a war that cut across the globalized financial system they operated in. Paul Warburg's resignation when the US entered the war is *consistent with* genuine conflict rather than secret coordination.

**Sources:** Federal Reserve History (official); Federal Reserve Bank of Minneapolis; Wikipedia (Paul Warburg, Max Warburg); Cambridge University Press (Financial History Review); Encyclopedia.com (Warburg, Max M)

### Finding 3: The Rothschild Network — Decline, Not Dominance
**Evidence Tier:** E2 (Ferguson's academic assessment); E3 (claims of coordinated dual-side financing)
**Confidence:** 0.80 (relative decline); 0.15 (deliberate dual-side financing)

Niall Ferguson's two-volume academic history (*The House of Rothschild*, based on unprecedented access to Rothschild family archives) documents that the Rothschilds remained the biggest bank in the world until World War I, but the war marked the beginning of their relative decline. The family's failure to establish themselves in the United States proved "fateful" — as financial power shifted from London to New York after 1914, Rothschild power waned.

The Rothschild Archive contains wartime correspondence between British and French family branches. The French branch (de Rothschild Frères, Paris) and British branch (N M Rothschild & Sons, London) operated on the Allied side. The Austrian branch (S M von Rothschild, Vienna) was in Central Powers territory. Ferguson documents that after WWI and the advent of income tax, governments had new means of raising funds that further reduced the Rothschilds' ability to influence events.

**Critical negative finding:** Claims that the Rothschilds "financed both sides" of WWI circulate widely but lack E1 documentation. The family had branches in multiple countries — this is structural legacy from the 19th century, not evidence of deliberate dual-side strategy. The Austrian branch's wartime activities are poorly documented compared to the London and Paris operations. Ferguson's assessment is that the war *damaged* Rothschild interests overall — inconsistent with a deliberate profit strategy.

**Sources:** Ferguson, Niall. *The House of Rothschild, Vol. 2: The World's Banker, 1849-1999* (Viking, 1999); Rothschild Archive official exhibitions; Britannica Money (Rothschild family)

### Finding 4: War Profiteering — Documented Scale
**Evidence Tier:** E1 (corporate records, Congressional testimony)
**Confidence:** 0.95

The Nye Committee (Senate Special Committee on Investigation of the Munitions Industry, 1934-1936) held 93 hearings, questioned 200+ witnesses including J.P. "Jack" Morgan and Pierre S. du Pont, and documented systematic profiteering:

| Company | Pre-War Annual Profit | Wartime Annual Profit | Increase |
|---------|----------------------|----------------------|----------|
| DuPont | $6 million | $58 million | ~950% |
| Bethlehem Steel | $6 million | $49 million | ~717% |

**DuPont** supplied 40% of the propellant powder used by the Allies. Revenues from powder and explosives soared from $25 million (1914) to $319 million (1918), totaling $1.245 billion over five years. The wartime profits funded DuPont's transformation from an explosives company into a diversified chemical corporation.

**Bethlehem Steel** (under Charles M. Schwab) had a virtual monopoly on certain Allied munitions contracts. Stock price rose from $30/share (1913) to $600/share (1915) to $700/share (1916) — a 2,233% increase from pre-war levels.

**Smedley Butler** (Major General, USMC, two-time Medal of Honor recipient) wrote in *War Is a Racket* (1935): "At least 21,000 new millionaires and billionaires were made in the United States during the World War." He documented that normal business profits of 6-12% expanded to wartime profits of 20-1,800%.

The US Treasury Department study found aggregate corporate net income nearly doubled from $4.7 billion (1913) to $8 billion (1917). Average profit rates for iron and steel firms rose from 7.4% to 28.7% between 1915 and 1917.

**Nye Committee conclusion:** Found "ample evidence that the armaments industry profited handsomely" but "little support for the theory that the industry had conspired to draw the nation into war." The committee documented bribery of foreign officials, selling weapons to both sides of international disputes, and covert undermining of disarmament conferences.

**Sources:** US Senate official history (senate.gov); Nye Committee records; Butler, Smedley. *War Is a Racket* (1935); PBS American Experience; Hagley Museum and Library Archives (DuPont records)

### Finding 5: Vickers-Krupp Patent Agreements — Cross-Faction Arms Manufacturing
**Evidence Tier:** E1 (patent records, government contracts, Trading with the Enemy Act records)
**Confidence:** 0.90

The most concretely documented cross-faction industrial connection: British arms manufacturer **Vickers** and German arms manufacturer **Krupp** maintained patent-sharing agreements from 1902 that continued into the war itself. Vickers used Krupp blueprints for time and percussion shell fuzes under license.

German troops were killed by shells with fuzes stamped "Krupp Patent Zünder" throughout WWI. Between 1914 and 1918, Vickers sold an estimated 14,139,000 fuzes of one type alone to the British government, valued at approximately £10,764,000 (approximately £200 million in 2005 money).

The contracts were only formally suspended in 1916 by the revised Trading with the Enemy Act. Even then, the Ministry of Munitions and Admiralty promised indemnities of up to £320,000 to Vickers against possible post-war Krupp patent claims — meaning the British government itself acknowledged the ongoing legal validity of these cross-faction commercial arrangements.

A predecessor arrangement existed since 1896: Harvey United Steel, an international cartel, counted directors from British, American, French, and German firms on its board, with Krupp earning royalties on each ton of armor plate produced.

**Sources:** Arming All Sides project (armingallsides.org.uk); Spear, Joanna. *The Business of Armaments: Armstrongs, Vickers and the International Arms Trade, 1855-1955* (Cambridge University Press)

### Finding 6: The Federal Reserve and War Finance Infrastructure
**Evidence Tier:** E1 (Federal Reserve records, Treasury records)
**Confidence:** 0.90 (institutional role); 0.30 (deliberate timing claim)

The Federal Reserve Act was signed December 23, 1913. The Federal Reserve System began operations in 1914 — the same year WWI began in Europe. By the time the US entered the war in April 1917, the Fed was fully organized and operational.

The Fed served as the institutional mechanism for selling Liberty Bonds and Victory Notes. Peak US government war indebtedness reached **$25.596 billion** (August 1919). The federal government financed the war through approximately one-third new taxes and two-thirds borrowing from the general population. The Federal Reserve and its member banks conducted the bond sales.

Paul Warburg, principal architect of the Federal Reserve, served on its board from 1914 and as Vice Chairman from 1916 — during the critical period when the institution was used to finance the war against his brother's country.

**Timing assessment:** Claims that the Federal Reserve was created *specifically* to finance WWI are widespread but lack documentary support. The Fed was designed to address the recurring banking panics of 1893 and 1907. The war began 7 months after the Fed's creation — insufficient time for deliberate planning. However, the Fed's existence *enabled* a scale of war finance that would have been impossible under the previous banking system. This is consequential whether or not it was intentional.

**Sources:** Federal Reserve History (official); Richmond Fed; Liberty Bond records (Wikipedia, citing Treasury records)

### Finding 7: Post-War Hegemonic Transfer — US Becomes Global Creditor
**Evidence Tier:** E1 (Treasury records, IMF data)
**Confidence:** 0.95

The single largest structural outcome of WWI was the transfer of financial hegemony from London to New York:

- **Pre-1914:** US was a net debtor to foreigners; Britain was the world's largest creditor nation
- **1917-1920:** US War and Navy departments spent $20 billion; Treasury extended $9.5 billion in loans to 11 Allied nations
- **By December 1922:** 20 nations owed the US Treasury $11.8 billion
- **Post-war:** Britain went from world's largest creditor (1914) to its largest debtor (1918)

New York replaced London as the world's leading financial center. The US began investing heavily abroad, particularly in Latin America, assuming Britain's traditional role as capital exporter.

**The Circular Flow (Dawes Plan, 1924):** US banks loaned money to Germany → Germany paid reparations to Britain and France → Britain and France repaid war debts to the US. Germany received 44.7 billion marks in loans while transferring only 16.8 billion marks to the Allies — a net transfer *to* Germany, financed by American investors. J.P. Morgan floated the initial Dawes Plan loan, which was quickly oversubscribed on Wall Street.

**Thomas W. Lamont** (Morgan partner) served as one of two US Treasury representatives at the Paris Peace Conference (1919). He helped determine German reparations and later drew up the Dawes Plan (1924) and Young Plan (1929). A Morgan partner thus shaped both the reparations structure and the lending mechanism that financed it — earning fees on both sides.

**Sources:** IMF (Debt and Entanglements Between the Wars); US State Department (Milestones: Dawes Plan); Encyclopedia.com (World War I War Debts); Britannica (Thomas W. Lamont)

### Finding 8: Kuhn, Loeb & Co. — The Neutrality Question
**Evidence Tier:** E2 (academic studies)
**Confidence:** 0.75 (firm neutrality); 0.25 (deliberate dual-side financing)

Kuhn, Loeb & Co. occupied a unique position: two partners (Paul M. Warburg and Felix M. Warburg) were brothers of Max Warburg, who financed the German war effort. Senior partner Jacob Schiff also had two brothers (Philip and Ludwig Schiff) active as bankers to the German government in Frankfurt.

Academic research (American Jewish Archives, 1997) documents that the firm was caught between conflicting pressures: Wall Street banks like Morgan pushed Kuhn, Loeb to participate in Allied war loans, while their German associates pushed them to abstain or participate in German financing.

**Resolution:** Kuhn, Loeb did not invest in Allied war loans. Schiff stated his refusal to lend to Russia reflected opposition to Tsarist persecution of Jews, not pro-German sympathies. The firm attempted neutrality but was unable to maintain it cleanly — family and professional ties crossed the battle lines.

**Sources:** Roberts, "The Case of Kuhn, Loeb and Company" (American Jewish Archives, 1997); "A Conflict of Loyalties: Kuhn, Loeb & Company and the First World War" (1984 academic paper); Wikipedia (Jacob Schiff)

### Finding 9: The Concert of Europe — Failure, Not Managed Restructuring
**Evidence Tier:** E2 (diplomatic history consensus)
**Confidence:** 0.85 (genuine failure); 0.10 (managed restructuring)

The hypothesis that the Concert of Europe's collapse was a "managed restructuring" rather than genuine failure finds virtually no support in mainstream diplomatic history. The Concert deteriorated gradually from the 1880s onward due to rival alliance systems (Triple Alliance vs. Triple Entente), rising nationalism, and imperial competition.

On the eve of WWI, British Foreign Minister Sir Edward Grey attempted to convene the Concert to prevent hostilities — and failed. His failure was a genuine diplomatic defeat, not theater. Grey's subsequent determination to establish a successor body (which became the League of Nations) is better explained by genuine alarm at the Concert's failure than by satisfaction at a planned outcome.

The financial beneficiaries of WWI (Morgan, DuPont, Bethlehem Steel) were not architects of pre-war European diplomacy. The causal chain runs from *political* failure to *financial* exploitation, not the reverse.

**Sources:** Wikipedia (Concert of Europe); EHNE Encyclopedia (Concert of Europe); Defence-in-Depth (King's College London)

## Negative Findings

### NF-1: "Rothschilds Financed Both Sides" — NOT SUPPORTED at E1/E2
The claim that the Rothschild family deliberately financed both the Allied and Central Powers as a coordinated strategy lacks documentary support. Ferguson's archival research shows the family had legacy branches in multiple countries (a 19th-century structure), but the war *damaged* Rothschild interests overall. The Austrian branch's wartime activities are poorly documented. The family's relative decline after WWI is inconsistent with a successful dual-side profit strategy. **Assessment: Popular claim, weak evidence.** Assign E3 at most; 0.15 confidence for intentional coordination.

### NF-2: "Federal Reserve Was Created to Finance WWI" — TIMING DOES NOT SUPPORT
The Fed was signed into law December 23, 1913. WWI began July 28, 1914 — seven months later. The Fed was designed to address the banking panics of 1893 and 1907, and its legislative history is well-documented as a response to domestic financial instability. The war began too soon after the Fed's creation for the institution to have been designed *for* war finance. The Fed did *enable* unprecedented war finance, but enabling ≠ designing. **Assessment: Conflation of consequence with intent.** E4 for deliberate design; 0.10 confidence.

### NF-3: "Concert of Europe Failure Was Managed Restructuring" — NO EVIDENCE
No credible diplomatic history supports the claim that the Concert of Europe's collapse was engineered by financial interests. The Concert deteriorated due to well-documented political dynamics: rival alliance systems, nationalism, imperial competition. Financial beneficiaries of WWI were not architects of pre-war European diplomacy. Grey's failed attempt to convene the Concert in 1914 is documented as genuine effort. **Assessment: Hypothesis lacks any evidentiary basis.** E4; 0.10 confidence.

### NF-4: "Warburg Brothers Coordinated Dual-Side Strategy" — NO DOCUMENTARY EVIDENCE
While the Warburg dual position is indisputable fact (E1), no correspondence, memoranda, or other documents have surfaced showing coordinated strategy between Paul and Max Warburg during the war. Paul's resignation from the Fed when the US entered the war is more consistent with genuine conflict than with secret coordination. Cambridge academic study documents the brothers' pre-war collaboration on German-American financial institutions but does not support wartime coordination claims. **Assessment: Fact (dual position) inflated to conspiracy (coordination) without evidence.** E1 for the fact; E4 for the coordination claim.

### NF-5: Nye Committee Did NOT Find Conspiracy to Enter War
Despite extensive investigation (93 hearings, 200+ witnesses), the Nye Committee found "little support for the theory that the industry had conspired to draw the nation into war." The committee found profiteering, bribery, and selling to both sides — but not a deliberate conspiracy to cause US entry. This is a critical distinction: exploitation of war ≠ causation of war. Many secondary sources elide this distinction. **Assessment: The committee's own conclusion must be honored even when it disappoints the conspiracy hypothesis.**

### NF-6: "21,000 New Millionaires" Claim — SOURCING UNCERTAIN
Butler's claim of 21,000 new millionaires/billionaires lacks clear sourcing methodology. Butler was a military officer, not an economist. The figure is widely cited but its original derivation is unclear. Downgraded to E2 with 0.70 confidence — directionally correct (massive wealth concentration occurred) but the specific number may be unreliable.

## Analytical Assessment: The Restructuring Hypothesis

The PKG-RESTRUCTURE central hypothesis asks whether major historical disruptions are "cartel maintenance operations." For WWI, the evidence supports a **more nuanced conclusion**:

**What IS documented (E1-E2):**
1. Financial elites profited enormously from the war (DuPont 950%, Bethlehem Steel 2,233% stock increase)
2. Morgan's financial entanglement with the Allies created structural incentives for US entry
3. Cross-faction commercial relationships existed before and during the war (Vickers-Krupp, Harvey United Steel)
4. Transnational banking families (Warburgs) had members on both sides
5. The post-war settlement was shaped by bankers (Lamont at Versailles, Morgan floating Dawes Plan loans)
6. The circular flow of reparations/loans enriched Wall Street intermediaries

**What is NOT documented (E3-E4):**
1. Deliberate engineering of the war by financial interests
2. Coordinated dual-side financing as conscious strategy
3. The Concert of Europe's failure as managed restructuring
4. The Federal Reserve as a war-finance instrument by design

**Structural assessment:** The pattern is **exploitation and shaping, not causation**. Financial elites did not start WWI, but they profited from it, influenced its prosecution, shaped the peace settlement, and designed the post-war financial architecture that enriched them further. This is consistent with the Prism cartel model at a **moderate confidence level (0.55-0.65)** — the same range as the PKG-EP finding. The system exhibits cartel-*like* behavior (coordinated extraction, regulatory capture, revolving door between finance and government) without requiring conscious conspiracy.

The strongest evidence for the restructuring hypothesis is not the war itself but the **post-war architecture**: a Morgan partner shaped reparations, Morgan floated the loans, and the circular flow (US loans → Germany → Allies → US) enriched the same intermediaries on every leg of the transaction. This is structural extraction, documentable at E1.
