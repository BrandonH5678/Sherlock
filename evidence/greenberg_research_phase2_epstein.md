### 2.3 Intelligence Assessment: The Departure

The one-day gap between the SEC case opening (March 10) and Epstein's resignation (March 12) is the most significant intelligence flag in this section. Three interpretations:

1. **Coincidence:** The Reg D fine/suspension had been building for weeks; resignation timing happened to overlap with the SEC inquiry opening. (LOW probability — securities firms monitor SEC actions in real time)

2. **Managed exit:** Bear Stearns management, learning of the SEC inquiry, arranged for Epstein's quiet departure before he could be further connected to the firm. The pre-existing Reg D violation provided a clean pretext. (MEDIUM-HIGH probability)

3. **Epstein self-exited:** Epstein, aware of the SEC inquiry, chose to distance himself from Bear Stearns voluntarily to avoid dragging the firm into an investigation that might scrutinize his own conduct. (MEDIUM probability — consistent with Epstein's later pattern of managed exits)

In all three interpretations, Greenberg — as CEO — would have been informed and would have participated in the decision.
