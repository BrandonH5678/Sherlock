## Edward J. DeBartolo Sr. — Phase 5: Wexner Nexus & Columbus Corridor
### Classification: OSINT Research | Date: 2026-02-24

---

## ANALYST NOTE

Phase 5 examines the documented and structural nexus between Edward J. DeBartolo Sr. and Leslie Wexner — the two wealthiest businessmen in Ohio during the 1970s-1990s, both with documented organized crime connections, both operating in overlapping Ohio commercial and political networks. The discovery of a **$1.7 billion joint business venture** (Carter-Hawley-Hale takeover bid, 1984 and 1986) provides concrete evidence of direct partnership, substantially upgrading what was previously only a "business and investment partnerships" reference in the Shapiro homicide file.

## 1. THE CARTER-HAWLEY-HALE JOINT VENTURE — PRIMARY DOCUMENTED CONNECTION

### 1.1 The Joint Takeover Bid

| Element | Detail | Evidence Tier |
|---------|--------|---------------|
| Target | Carter Hawley Hale Stores Inc. — LA-based department store chain owning The Broadway, Neiman-Marcus, Bergdorf Goodman, Emporium, and Contempo Casuals | E1 (LA Times reporting) |
| Year(s) | 1984 (first bid) and 1986 (second bid) | E2 |
| Bid value | Approximately $1.7 billion | E2 (Yahoo Finance; multiple sources) |
| Structure | Joint bid: DeBartolo Corp. (real estate/retail developer) + The Limited (Wexner) | E2 |
| LA Times quote (1986) | "The takeover offer for Carter Hawley brings together the two richest men in Ohio." | E2 (LA Times 1986, sourced via Yahoo Finance/secondary) |
| Outcome | Failed — Carter Hawley successfully resisted the takeover | E2 |
| Significance | This was not a casual or arms-length transaction. A $1.7B coordinated takeover bid requires intensive mutual due diligence, shared legal structure, combined financing arrangements, joint governance agreements, and sustained high-level personal collaboration between DeBartolo Sr. and Wexner. | Analytical |

### 1.2 Why Carter-Hawley-Hale Was Strategically Attractive to Both

The joint bid logic is transparent in hindsight:
- **DeBartolo's interest:** Carter Hawley operated major department stores — The Broadway, Emporium — that were anchor tenants in large regional malls. Acquiring anchor-tenant chains directly would give DeBartolo vertically integrated control of anchor economics in his mall portfolio.
- **Wexner's interest:** Acquiring luxury retail brands (Neiman-Marcus, Bergdorf Goodman) would dramatically expand The Limited's brand portfolio beyond mass-market apparel. Wexner had demonstrated his acquisition playbook (Victoria's Secret $1M → $1B; Lane Bryant, Lerner, Henri Bendel, A&F).
- **Joint structure:** DeBartolo's real estate expertise and capital + Wexner's retail operating expertise = natural complementarity for a department store chain acquisition that included both real estate (stores in malls) and retail operations.

**Assessment:** The Carter-Hawley-Hale joint bid represents a sophisticated strategic partnership between two businessmen who clearly had pre-existing trust and business familiarity sufficient to undertake a $1.7B joint venture. This confirms the Shapiro file's reference to "well-known history of business and investment partnerships" as accurate. The relationship was substantial, not incidental.

**Evidence tier: E2** (well-documented in contemporaneous press; the LA Times quote is primary; the full original article is not directly accessible but is cited in multiple downstream sources)

## 2. THE SHAPIRO MURDER FILE — DeBARTOLO-WEXNER AS SHARED ORGANIZED CRIME NEXUS

### 2.1 The 1991 Columbus Police Document

The 1991 Columbus Police Department "Shapiro Homicide Investigation: Analysis and Hypothesis" is the most important law enforcement document linking DeBartolo and Wexner in a shared organized crime context.

| Element | Detail | Evidence Tier |
|---------|--------|---------------|
| Document | "Shapiro Homicide Investigation: Analysis and Hypothesis," Columbus Police Organized Crime Bureau, June 1991 | E1 (digitized; available at archive.org, "shapiromurderfilecomplete1") |
| Subject | Murder of Arthur L. Shapiro, Columbus attorney, shot twice in the head at a Columbus cemetery, March 6, 1985 | E1 (murder case) |
| Shapiro's role | Personal attorney to Leslie Wexner; also connected to DeBartolo Sr. via Columbus business networks | E2 |
| Joint framing | The report described both DeBartolo and Wexner's business network in an organized crime context and cited their "well-known history of business and investment partnerships" | E1 (document) |
| Organized crime finding | "Strong marks of a La Cosa Nostra (L.C.N.) 'hit'" per Columbus PD OCB | E1 (document) |
| DeBartolo characterization | "Associate of the Genovese-LaRocca crime family in Pittsburgh" (sourced from Pennsylvania Crime Commission) | E2 (document drawing on PCC files) |
| Chief's suppression | Columbus Police Chief James G. Jackson ordered the document destroyed June 1991; confronted by Channel 4 News: "I thought I got rid of it" and termed it "scandalous" | E2 (Unlimited Hangout / freepress.org) |
| Survival and availability | Document survived; digitized; hosted at archive.org | E1-accessible |

### 2.2 Arthur Shapiro: The Wexner-DeBartolo Bridge Figure

Arthur Shapiro was a Columbus attorney at Schwartz, Shapiro, Kelm & Warren who served as Wexner's personal attorney. He was murdered on March 6, 1985 — one day before he was scheduled to appear before a grand jury. The murder bore the hallmarks of a professional assassination: two shots to the head at a cemetery in Columbus.

| Question | Assessment | Evidence Tier |
|----------|------------|---------------|
| Was Shapiro Wexner's personal attorney? | Confirmed — multiple sources establish this | E2 |
| Did Shapiro have connections to DeBartolo Sr.? | Probable — Columbus PD OCB report placed him within the DeBartolo-Wexner organized crime nexus | E2 (document) |
| Was Shapiro killed to prevent grand jury testimony? | The murder occurred the day before his scheduled grand jury appearance — extremely unlikely to be coincidence | E2 (analytical) |
| Who ordered Shapiro's death? | Columbus PD initially suspected Berry Kessler, an accountant with criminal history; Kessler was ultimately convicted of murder-for-hire | E1 (court records) |
| Who hired Kessler? | Not established in the public record; the murder-for-hire chain has not been traced to a principal in publicly available court documents | E1 (gap) |

### 2.3 What the Suppression of the Shapiro File Tells Us

Columbus Police Chief James G. Jackson's order to destroy an official homicide investigation document — and his reaction when confronted ("I thought I got rid of it. It's scandalous.") — is the single strongest evidence that DeBartolo and Wexner's organized crime connections were known at the executive level of Ohio law enforcement and considered politically too dangerous to pursue.

The Chief's statement reflects:
1. Personal knowledge of the document's contents (he called it "scandalous")
2. Active destruction of an official public record (a criminal act)
3. Awareness that the organized crime allegations were politically sensitive
4. Familiarity with DeBartolo and Wexner's prominence in Ohio political and business networks

**Assessment:** This is not a case of administrative mishandling. A sitting Columbus Police Chief personally ordered the destruction of a murder investigation document linking Ohio's two wealthiest men to organized crime. The political protection of DeBartolo and Wexner appears to have extended to the command level of Columbus law enforcement.

## 3. COMMERCIAL NEXUS — THE LIMITED IN DeBARTOLO MALLS

### 3.1 Structural Relationship

| Element | Detail | Evidence Tier |
|---------|--------|---------------|
| DeBartolo's portfolio | 200+ shopping centers by peak (100 regional malls + 100+ community centers); nation's largest mall developer | E1 |
| The Limited's footprint | The Limited, Inc. operated thousands of mall-based retail locations across the US under multiple brands (The Limited, Victoria's Secret, Lane Bryant, Lerner New York, Express, Abercrombie & Fitch) | E1 |
| Structural intersection | The Limited was one of the most important and ubiquitous retail anchor/co-anchor tenants in the American mall sector during the 1980s-1990s. DeBartolo was the nation's largest mall developer in the same period. Their commercial relationship as developer and major tenant is structurally near-certain. | E2 (structural inference) |
| Specific DeBartolo-Limited lease documents | No specific lease agreements have been identified in open-source research. Neither DeBartolo Corp. nor The Limited publicly reported property-by-property tenant lists in a consolidated format accessible to researchers. | UNVERIFIED (specific transactions) |

**Assessment:** The structural commercial relationship between DeBartolo (mall developer) and Wexner (dominant mall retailer) almost certainly involved The Limited stores as tenants in DeBartolo malls. This is the foundational commercial rationale for the $1.7B Carter-Hawley-Hale joint venture — they were already commercial partners and understood each other's business models. However, specific lease documentation has not been identified in available sources.

### 3.2 The Carter-Hawley-Hale Bid as Confirmation of Commercial Understanding

The joint takeover bid is itself evidence of deep commercial familiarity. Bidding jointly for an $1.7B retail/real estate portfolio requires:
- Mutual knowledge of each other's operating capabilities and financial standing
- Established trust from prior business dealings
- Shared strategic vision for how to operate the acquired assets
- Compatible due diligence and governance frameworks

This level of commercial intimacy is not typically established through a single prior transaction. The Carter-Hawley-Hale joint bid most plausibly reflects a pre-existing relationship of the kind that would arise from years of DeBartolo-Limited lease-level commercial dealings.

## 4. OHIO ORGANIZED CRIME ECOSYSTEM — THE CORRIDOR

### 4.1 The Youngstown-Pittsburgh-Cleveland Triangle

DeBartolo operated in Youngstown — the apex of one of the most thoroughly organized-crime-penetrated environments in American history. During the period when DeBartolo established his mall empire (1940s-1970s), Youngstown was contested territory between the Cleveland and Pittsburgh crime families.

| Geography | Organization | Control | Evidence Tier |
|-----------|-------------|---------|---------------|
| Youngstown core | Cleveland family (Carabbia brothers) + Pittsburgh family (Prato, Naples) | Divided/contested | E1 (court records) |
| Pittsburgh family advance | Pittsburgh family expanded aggressively into Youngstown in 1960s-1970s | Territorial expansion | E1 (court records) |
| Youngstown war | Pittsburgh-Cleveland conflict, approximately 1978-1981; 12+ murders | War for control | E1 (court records, convictions) |
| Resolution | Pittsburgh family won; Cleveland Carabbia brothers eliminated (Charlie Carabbia murdered Dec. 1980) | Pittsburgh dominant | E1 (court records) |
| DeBartolo in the war | DeBartolo Sr.'s documented relationship was with the Carabbia brothers (Cleveland side); he simultaneously owned Pittsburgh Penguins (Pittsburgh side) from 1977 | Dual-family hedging or coincidence | E2 |

### 4.2 Columbus Organized Crime Ecosystem

Les Wexner built his empire in Columbus — a separate Ohio organized crime ecosystem from Youngstown. The key organized crime node connecting Wexner to the organized crime ecosystem was Walsh Trucking:

| Element | Detail | Evidence Tier |
|---------|--------|---------------|
| Walsh Trucking | Handled 90%+ of The Limited's business; co-located at One Limited Parkway, Columbus | E1 (RICO indictment) |
| RICO prosecution | Walsh Trucking prosecuted in 1988 RICO case by Samuel Alito Jr. (then US Attorney) | E1 (court records) |
| Fat Tony Salerno | Anthony "Fat Tony" Salerno (Genovese family boss, NYC) was an unindicted co-conspirator in the Walsh RICO case | E1 (court records) |
| DeBartolo's Genovese connection | DeBartolo characterized as "associate of the Genovese-LaRocca crime family in Pittsburgh" | E2 (Penn Crime Commission / Columbus PD) |
| Structural nexus | Both DeBartolo (Genovese-LaRocca) and Wexner's logistics partner Walsh (Fat Tony Salerno, Genovese boss) had connections to the Genovese crime family — the same organization | E2 (analytical) |

### 4.3 The Genovese Family Thread

The Genovese family appears as a common thread:
1. DeBartolo characterized as "Genovese-LaRocca associate" (Pennsylvania Crime Commission)
2. Walsh Trucking RICO implicates Fat Tony Salerno (Genovese boss, NYC) as co-conspirator
3. The Pittsburgh family's alternative designation was "Genovese-LaRocca" — reflecting its organizational lineage from the NYC Genovese family

This does not establish that DeBartolo and Wexner operated under a common Genovese command structure — the Genovese family was large and its "associates" across the country were not a unified network. But it does mean that organized crime documentation of both figures traces to the same national crime family.

**Evidence tier: E2** (documented for both parties separately; Genovese family as common denominator is analytical inference)

### 4.4 Was There a Youngstown-Columbus Organized Crime Corridor?

The organized crime literature documents extensive Pittsburgh family operations extending from Pittsburgh → Youngstown → further. Columbus is further south and west — it was not primary Pittsburgh family territory. However:

| Aspect | Assessment | Evidence Tier |
|--------|------------|---------------|
| Walsh RICO Salerno connection | Fat Tony Salerno (NYC) having interests in Columbus trucking is more consistent with national Genovese reach than a Youngstown-Columbus corridor | E1 (RICO case) |
| DeBartolo building in Columbus area | DeBartolo built multiple malls in Ohio including the Randall Park Mall (Cleveland area) — his operations were statewide | E2 |
| Documented Youngstown-Columbus corridor | No specific law enforcement document describes a Youngstown-Columbus organized crime corridor. The organized crime ecosystems were largely separate networks connected through national family ties, not a geographic corridor. | NEGATIVE (as geographic corridor) |
| Functional conclusion | Ohio's organized crime connections operated through national family structures (Genovese, primarily), not through a state-level corridor. DeBartolo and Wexner both intersected the Genovese network from different geographic nodes (Youngstown/Pittsburgh vs. Columbus). | E2 (analytical) |

### 5.2 Ohio Political Power Context

Both DeBartolo Sr. and Wexner wielded substantial Ohio political influence:
- DeBartolo's influence was exercised primarily through Youngstown-area politics, where organized crime and political corruption were thoroughly intertwined (Congressman James Traficant, convicted, operated in this ecosystem)
- Wexner's influence operated through Columbus-based Democratic and then Republican mainstream political channels
- Both had Ohio-wide standing as the wealthiest businessmen in the state — confirmed by LA Times' "two richest men in Ohio" (1986)

Neither DeBartolo Sr. nor Wexner's political relationships have been specifically documented as overlapping. Their political networks appear to have been parallel but geographically distinct (Youngstown vs. Columbus).

### 6.3 The Core Analytical Finding

The DeBartolo-Wexner nexus is best characterized as a **dual-layer relationship**:

**Layer 1 (Commercial):** The two wealthiest men in Ohio in the 1980s were naturally drawn into business partnership — DeBartolo as mall developer/owner, Wexner as dominant mall retailer. The Carter-Hawley-Hale joint bid ($1.7B) is the most concrete documented expression of this commercial partnership. Their combined resources and complementary expertise made them natural co-investors for retail/real estate acquisition targets.

**Layer 2 (Organized Crime Ecosystem Overlap):** Both operated in organized crime ecosystems that traced to the Genovese crime family — DeBartolo through the Genovese-LaRocca Pittsburgh family (Youngstown); Wexner through Walsh Trucking's Fat Tony Salerno co-conspirator connection (Columbus). The Shapiro murder connects both men in a single law enforcement document that characterized their relationship in an organized crime context and was then ordered destroyed by the Columbus Police Chief.

**The combined assessment:** DeBartolo and Wexner were simultaneously legitimate business partners, Ohio's two wealthiest men, and individuals whose organized crime connections independently traced to the Genovese family. Whether their organized crime relationships ever overlapped operationally — whether they were aware of each other's mob connections, whether they leveraged those connections jointly — is not established in the public record. The Shapiro murder file suggests that Columbus law enforcement believed the answer was yes, but that file's evidence base and the Chief's suppression of it prevent a definitive assessment.

## RESEARCH AGENT ADDENDUM — VERBATIM SHAPIRO FILE QUOTES (E1)

The following verbatim passages from the 1991 Columbus Police "Shapiro Homicide Investigation: Analysis and Hypothesis" were recovered via the archive.org digitized primary source (`shapiromurderfilecomplete1`). These upgrade several D5 claims from E2 to E1.

### Key Verbatim Passages:

**On the DeBartolo-Wexner partnership (E1):**
> "Leslie Wexner and the Youngstown, Ohio developer, Edward J. DeBartolo, have a long history of partnership in business dealings."

**On organized crime characterization (E1):**
> "Both DeBartolo and Walsh have been identified as associates of the Genovese-LaRocca crime family in Pittsburgh."

**On Wexner's organized crime exposure (E1):**
> "From the predicate facts presented, it appears that Les Wexner had established contact with associates reputed to be organized crime figures, one of whom was a **major investment partner** and another was using The Limited headquarters as a mailing address."

The "major investment partner" identified in context = DeBartolo. The person using The Limited headquarters as a mailing address = Frank Walsh (National Westminster Bank subpoena notices addressed to "Frank Walsh Financial Resources at One Limited Parkway").

**Businesses identified with "unusual interactive relations" to The Limited (E1):**
- The Limited (Wexner)
- Walsh Trucking Company
- Schwartz, Kelm, Warren and Rubenstein (The Limited's law firm, formerly Shapiro's)
- Omni Oil Company
- **The Edward DeBartolo Company of Youngstown, Ohio**
- John W. Kessler (Columbus developer)
- Major Chord Jazz Club (Jerry Hammond)

**On Shapiro's grand jury context (E1):**
The grand jury was an **IRS** investigation into "unreported income and questionable tax shelters" — specifically, not just a generic grand jury but an IRS tax investigation whose suppression had financial motivation.

**Carter-Hawley-Hale verbatim (E1):**
> "In 1984, DeBartolo and Wexner were partners in a two-month takeover war against Carter-Hawley-Hale stores... Two-and-a-half years later, in November 1986, The Limited and the Edward J. DeBartolo Corporation again attempted a hostile takeover of Carter-Hawley-Hale stores with a $1.8 billion cash offer."

### Police Chief Jackson's Formal Charges (E2 upgrade):

Chief Jackson was not merely confronted on camera — he was **formally charged in 1996 with "improper disposal of a public record"** for ordering the Shapiro file destroyed. This is a criminal charge, not merely an embarrassing confrontation. Source: Bob Fitrakis, Columbus Alive/Freepress.org (1998).

### Walsh RICO Addendum (E1 — UPI newswire, court records):

- Walsh indicted May 16, 1988 (prosecuted by then-US Attorney Samuel A. Alito Jr.)
- Walsh pled guilty; received **four-year prison sentence in 1990** — confirming conviction
- Fat Tony Salerno named as **unindicted co-conspirator** (same indictment that also named Matthew "Matty the Horse" Ianniello and the Provenzano brothers)
- Walsh maintained "Frank Walsh Financial Resources" mailing address at One Limited Parkway while under organized crime investigation

Source: UPI newswire, "Trucker charged with paying off Teamsters local," May 16, 1988; UPI, "Mobster 'Fat Tony' Salerno indicted," May 18, 1988.
