## 4. Apollo and the 2008 Financial Crisis

### 4.1 Apollo's Performance During the Crisis

The 2008 financial crisis was both a challenge and an opportunity for Apollo:

**Losses:**
- Apollo's leveraged portfolio companies suffered from the credit freeze
- Multiple Apollo portfolio companies entered distressed or bankruptcy proceedings (Hexion/Huntsman deal collapsed; others struggled with debt loads)
- Apollo's private equity fund valuations declined significantly from 2007 peaks
- Apollo Fund VI (2006 vintage, ~$10B) was marked down by an estimated 50%+ at the crisis nadir (E2 -- financial press)

**Opportunities:**
- Apollo's distressed debt expertise (inherited from Drexel) positioned it perfectly to buy assets during the crisis
- The firm raised and deployed significant capital into distressed credit markets
- Apollo acquired debt at steep discounts and profited from the recovery
- The crisis created the conditions for Apollo to launch Athene (2009), which became its largest affiliate

### 4.2 Exposure to Bear Stearns Collapse (Target 55)

- Apollo did not have significant documented direct exposure to the Bear Stearns collapse in March 2008 (E2)
- Apollo's credit funds may have held Bear Stearns-originated securities (CDOs, mortgage-backed securities), but any such holdings would have been small relative to portfolio size
- The Bear Stearns collapse's primary impact on Apollo was indirect: it triggered the broader credit market freeze that affected all leveraged private equity firms
- No evidence of Apollo involvement in the Bear Stearns rescue by JPMorgan Chase (E2)

**PKG-911 relevance of Bear Stearns collapse for Apollo:** Minimal direct relevance. The Sherlock-relevant Bear Stearns connections (BCCI, Epstein, Greenberg) run through the bank itself, not through Apollo's portfolio. (E2)

### 4.3 Exposure to Lehman Brothers Collapse
