## 2. The Blackwater Protection Controversy

This section constitutes the central analytical finding of Phase 3. Howard Krongard is documented to have blocked or impeded investigations into multiple State Department contractor misconduct allegations, with Blackwater Worldwide as the most prominent subject.

### 2.1 The Waxman Investigation: Timeline of Events

**Phase 1: Whistleblower Complaints (Pre-September 2007)**

Seven current and former officials in the Inspector General's office expressed concerns about Krongard's conduct and his failure to investigate credible reports of wasteful spending and procurement fraud, especially in Iraq and Afghanistan. Two former senior officials allowed their names to be used publicly. (Source: Washington Post, Sept 18, 2007 — E1)

**Phase 2: The Waxman Letter (September 18, 2007)**

Rep. Henry Waxman, chairman of the House Committee on Oversight and Government Reform, issued a 14-page letter charging Krongard with actively impeding probes into waste and corruption related to the war in Iraq and other matters. (Source: Washington Post; House Oversight Committee — E1)

The letter documented the following specific allegations:

| Investigation Blocked | Details | Evidence Grade |
|----------------------|---------|----------------|
| Baghdad Embassy Construction | $736 million embassy, running $144 million over cost; Krongard refused staff recommendations to investigate waste and fraud | E1 (committee documentation) |
| Blackwater Arms Smuggling | Justice Department investigation into Blackwater employees illegally smuggling weapons to Iraq; weapons possibly sold on black market to PKK (Kurdistan Workers Party) | E1 (internal emails obtained) |
| DynCorp Procurement Fraud | $1 billion contract for training Iraqi and Afghan police; Krongard refused to pursue procurement fraud charges | E1 (committee documentation) |
| General Iraq/Afghanistan Oversight | Krongard refused to send investigators to Iraq and Afghanistan to investigate $3 billion in State Dept contracts | E1 (whistleblower testimony) |

**Phase 3: The Arms Smuggling Email (July 11, 2007)**

The committee obtained an internal email from Krongard dated July 11, 2007, directing his staff to stop immediately any work on Blackwater-related contracts until he received a briefing. This email constitutes direct documentary evidence of interference with an active Justice Department criminal investigation. (Source: Washington Post; House Oversight Committee — E1)

On July 31, 2007, Justice Department attorneys briefed Howard Krongard on the Blackwater case. According to a State Department agent present, Krongard "cited numerous reasons his investigators should not be involved in the investigation" and said the Justice Department was "wasting his time." (Source: Washington Post — E1, quoting agent testimony)

The arms smuggling investigation concerned allegations that Blackwater employees had illegally smuggled automatic weapons into Iraq that may have ended up in the hands of the PKK, a U.S.-designated terrorist organization. Turkish authorities had protested to the U.S. in July 2007 that they had seized American arms from PKK rebels. (Source: NBC News; CBS News — E1)

**Phase 4: Witness Intimidation (September 28, 2007)**

In a three-page follow-up letter dated September 28, 2007, Waxman informed Krongard that allegations of witness intimidation had been made against Krongard's staff:

- Aides to Krongard threatened **two** U.S. State Department investigators with retaliation, including **termination of their jobs**, if they cooperated with congressional investigations into Blackwater and Krongard
- Krongard's congressional liaison told one investigator that "Howard could fire him"
- Multiple career investigators sought protection under the Whistleblower Protection Act

(Source: Washington Post, Sept 28, 2007; House report — E1)

**Phase 5: The Nisour Square Massacre (September 16, 2007)**

Two days before Waxman's initial letter, Blackwater contractors killed 17 and injured 20 Iraqi civilians in Nisour Square, Baghdad. This became the FBI's most comprehensive and expensive criminal investigation since 9/11.

**CRITICAL TIMING:** The Nisour Square massacre occurred on September 16, 2007. Waxman's 14-page letter documenting Krongard's obstruction of Blackwater investigations was issued September 18, 2007 — just two days later. The IG who should have been investigating Blackwater's conduct had been actively blocking such investigations for months. (E1 — timing confirmed from multiple sources)

### 2.2 The November 14, 2007 Congressional Hearing

This hearing before the House Committee on Oversight and Government Reform, chaired by Rep. Henry Waxman, produced one of the most dramatic moments in modern congressional oversight history.

**The Sequence of Events:**

1. **The Question:** Krongard was asked whether his brother Alvin "Buzzy" Krongard had any connection to Blackwater Worldwide.

2. **The Denial (Under Oath):** Krongard stated: *"I can tell you, very frankly, I am not aware of any financial interest or position he has with respect to Blackwater... I do not believe it is true that he is a member of the advisory board that you stated. And that's something I think I need to say."* (Source: Congressional testimony, multiple press accounts — E1)

3. **The Documentary Confrontation:** Democrats produced two pieces of documentary evidence:
   - A **July 26, 2007 letter** from Blackwater founder and CEO Erik Prince to A.B. Krongard, inviting him to join Blackwater's advisory board
   - A **September 5, 2007 letter** from Prince welcoming Krongard to the board
   - Rep. Elijah Cummings stated that Buzzy Krongard had been expected to attend a Blackwater board meeting **earlier that week**

4. **The Recess and Phone Call:** Krongard called a recess. During the break, he telephoned his brother. (E1 — confirmed by multiple press accounts)

5. **The Admission:** Krongard returned and stated he had "learned of this conflict of interest for the first time" during the phone call. He said: *"I'm not my brother's keeper, and we don't discuss our business with each other."* (Source: CNN, Nov 14, 2007 — E1)

6. **The Recusal:** Krongard stated on the record: *"I hereby recuse myself from any matters having to do with Blackwater."* (Source: Congressional testimony — E1)

### 2.3 The Contradicted Testimony

After the hearing, a critical contradiction emerged:

- **Howard's Claim:** He did not know his brother was on Blackwater's advisory board until the hearing.
- **Buzzy's Claim (Post-Hearing):** Buzzy Krongard told reporter Spencer Ackerman (Talking Points Memo): *"I had told my brother I was going on the advisory board."* He added: *"I stand by what I told my brother."*

Chairman Waxman issued a **memorandum on November 16, 2007** summarizing the discrepancies between the two brothers' recollections, stating the contradictions raise "serious questions about the veracity of Howard Krongard's testimony before the committee." (Source: Waxman memorandum; Baltimore Sun, Nov 20, 2007 — E1)

**ANALYTICAL ASSESSMENT:** If Buzzy Krongard told Howard about the Blackwater advisory board position — as Buzzy publicly asserted — then Howard Krongard testified falsely under oath before Congress. This would constitute perjury. The discrepancy was never formally resolved. Confidence: **0.70** that Howard knew before the hearing (based on Buzzy's public contradiction and the brothers' documented, if infrequent, contact). (E2 — corroborated by Buzzy's post-hearing statements, but no independent witness)

## 7. Overall Assessment

### 7.1 Analytical Significance of Howard Krongard's IG Tenure

Howard Krongard's 32-month tenure as State Department Inspector General (May 2005 - January 2008) represents one of the most documented cases of IG capture in modern U.S. government history. The evidence establishes:

1. **Systematic Obstruction of Oversight:** At least three major investigations (Blackwater arms smuggling, Baghdad Embassy fraud, DynCorp procurement fraud) were blocked or impeded, not as isolated incidents, but as a pattern. (E1)

2. **Witness Intimidation:** Career federal investigators were threatened with termination for cooperating with congressional oversight — a direct attack on the whistleblower protection framework. (E1)

3. **Conflict of Interest:** The IG responsible for investigating Blackwater had a brother on Blackwater's advisory board. When exposed, Krongard denied knowledge under oath, a denial subsequently contradicted by his brother's public statements. (E1)

4. **Zero Accountability:** Despite all of the above, Krongard faced no prosecution, no DOJ referral, no censure, and no formal consequences beyond forced resignation. (E1)

### 7.2 Deliberate vs. Structural vs. Coincidental

| Hypothesis | Assessment | Confidence |
|-----------|-----------|------------|
| **H1: Coincidental** — Howard independently decided to block investigations; Buzzy independently joined Blackwater board; no coordination | Inconsistent with evidence pattern. The July 11 email blocking Blackwater investigation preceded the July 26 Prince-to-Buzzy invitation by 15 days. Seven whistleblowers independently reported obstruction. | **0.10** |
| **H2: Structural** — Family network position created natural protective alignment without explicit coordination; brothers' careers in overlapping domains produced structural protection for Blackwater | Partially consistent. The brothers' claimed estrangement and infrequent contact support this reading. Structural benefit occurred regardless of intent. | **0.45** |
| **H3: Deliberate** — Howard's blocking of Blackwater investigations was coordinated with or motivated by awareness of Buzzy's financial and career interests in Blackwater's viability | Partially consistent. Buzzy publicly claimed he told Howard about the Blackwater board position. The breadth of Howard's obstruction (not just Blackwater, but all Iraq contractor investigations) may indicate ideological alignment rather than specific coordination. The broader pattern suggests a political appointee protecting the administration's contractor ecosystem, with family interest as an additional motive. | **0.40** |

**MOST PROBABLE ASSESSMENT:** The Blackwater protection was primarily political (Bush administration appointee protecting wartime contractors from oversight) with a family-network overlay (Buzzy's Blackwater interests creating additional motive). This combination of political and personal motives is more probable than either pure coincidence or pure conspiracy. Confidence: **0.65** for combined political-plus-family motivation. (E2)

### 7.3 Institutional Implications

The Krongard IG episode demonstrates a structural vulnerability in the Inspector General system: presidential appointees with no government experience can be placed in oversight roles where their primary loyalty is to the appointing administration rather than to oversight independence. When combined with family network conflicts of interest, the result is a comprehensive failure of accountability.

This structural vulnerability was exploited during the War on Terror to shield private military contractors — particularly Blackwater — from oversight at the precise moment when those contractors were involved in their most serious misconduct (Nisour Square, arms smuggling, procurement fraud).
